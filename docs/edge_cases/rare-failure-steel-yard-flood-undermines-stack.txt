Heavy rain eroded soil beneath a stack of long products, causing one support to settle. The stack was cordoned and unloaded only under the yard lifting plan. Record stack, support, rain, settlement, material, and action. Do not climb or pull material from a leaning stack. Inspect drainage, ground bearing, chocks, and adjacent stacks after flooding.  The event was added to handover because it may recur under similar material, weather, load, or maintenance conditions. Protect people first, preserve evidence, and identify the early sign, safe boundary, escalation point, and proof required before normal operation. Do not call an unusual alarm harmless merely because the plant recovered once. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
