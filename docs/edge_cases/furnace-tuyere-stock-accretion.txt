A tuyere camera showed a narrowing raceway and unstable flame after a burden change. Cooling flow was normal, but a stock accretion had formed near the tuyere. The furnace controller reduced the affected injection and reviewed blast, fuel, and burden data. No one approached the tuyere beyond the protected observation point. Record tuyere, image, cooling, injection, blast condition, and recovery. A dark or constricted tuyere may be process buildup, camera fouling, or damage; compare neighboring tuyeres before deciding. Changes should be gradual and documented.  The event was added to handover because it may recur under similar material, weather, load, or maintenance conditions. Protect people first, preserve evidence, and identify the early sign, safe boundary, escalation point, and proof required before normal operation. Do not call an unusual alarm harmless merely because the plant recovered once. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
