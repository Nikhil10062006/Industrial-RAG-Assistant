Scenario: Derailment of a fully loaded torpedo car (hot metal rail car) on the switchyard curve between the Blast Furnace and Melt Shop.
Action Taken: A 400-ton car jumping the tracks is a crisis. The immediate risk isn't just the derailment; it's the hot metal cooling inside. If the metal solidifies inside the torpedo (a "skull"), the car is essentially destroyed. We had a maximum window of 12 hours. The heavy recovery team used 500-ton hydraulic jacks to re-rail the bogeys.
Lesson Learned: While waiting for the recovery crew, we rigged up industrial water hoses to spray the external steel shell of the torpedo car continuously. This seems counter-intuitive, but chilling the steel shell shrinks it slightly and protects the structural integrity of the car from the immense heat of the stagnant iron inside. Never let a derailed hot car sit without shell cooling.
