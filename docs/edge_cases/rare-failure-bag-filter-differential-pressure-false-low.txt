A filter differential-pressure signal fell to near zero while dust emissions increased. The high-side impulse line had cracked and both transmitter ports saw the same pressure. The signal was treated as invalid, filter condition inspected, and tubing replaced. Record pressure, dust, impulse lines, fan state, and data gap. A low differential pressure can mean a broken measurement path or damaged bags, not a clean filter.  The event was added to handover because it may recur under similar material, weather, load, or maintenance conditions. Protect people first, preserve evidence, and identify the early sign, safe boundary, escalation point, and proof required before normal operation. Do not call an unusual alarm harmless merely because the plant recovered once. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
