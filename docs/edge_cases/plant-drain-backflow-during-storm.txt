During a severe storm, water backed up through a plant drain and entered a chemical area. The flap valve was jammed by scale and the receiving channel was full. The area was barricaded, chemicals lifted or isolated, and the valve cleaned after the water level fell. Record rainfall, drain level, valve condition, chemical exposure, and cleanup. A drain route is not automatically one-way. Inspect backflow protection before monsoon and keep chemical storage above the flood level.  The event was added to handover because it may recur under similar material, weather, load, or maintenance conditions. Protect people first, preserve evidence, and identify the early sign, safe boundary, escalation point, and proof required before normal operation. Do not call an unusual alarm harmless merely because the plant recovered once. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
