A pusher machine brake drifted approximately 50 mm during a stationary hold test. The machine display showed zero speed and the brake pressure switch was healthy. Inspection found contamination on the brake disc and a worn actuator seal. The machine was isolated, the rail route blocked, and the brake repaired before any oven movement. Record brake pressure, drift distance, hold time, rail condition, actuator leakage, and test result. Veteran note: a zero-speed display does not prove a stopped machine. Watch the physical machine and measure creep. A pusher carrying a hot charge must not be trusted after a short drift. Include brake-hold tests after washdown, rain, or maintenance near the wheels.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
