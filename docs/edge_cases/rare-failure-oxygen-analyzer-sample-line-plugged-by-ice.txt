An oxygen analyzer showed a stable normal value while the sample-line flow had fallen nearly to zero during a cold night. Moisture in the line had frozen near a low point, isolating the analyzer from the process. The analyzer was treated as invalid, and product quality was verified with the approved independent sample. The line was warmed safely, drained, insulated, and flow alarmed. Record ambient temperature, sample flow, analyzer value, trap condition, and recovery time. Veteran note: a stable analyzer can be stale data if the sample path is blocked. Check sample flow and response time, not only calibration date. Do not use an open flame to thaw an oxygen-service line.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
