An empty crane hook was found with a damaged latch during travel toward the ladle bay. No load was suspended, but the hook passed over a walkway before the defect was noticed. The crane was stopped, isolated, and inspected. The latch had been bent during a previous contact and the defect was not recorded. The route was reviewed and the hook inspection was added to the pre-use check. Record crane, hook, latch condition, route, and corrective action. Lesson: an empty hook can still strike people or equipment, and a damaged latch becomes critical when the next load is taken. Do not use a hook because the crane is only traveling empty. Every travel begins with a lifting-equipment check.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
