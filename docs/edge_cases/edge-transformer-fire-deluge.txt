Scenario: A catastrophic internal fault in a 33kV/11kV Main Step-Down Transformer ruptured the tank, igniting 15,000 liters of mineral oil.
Action Taken: The automated water deluge system activated, but the high-pressure water spray actually made the fire worse. The solid streams of water sank beneath the burning oil, boiled instantly into steam, and caused a massive "slop-over," throwing a fireball 30 meters into the air and catching the adjacent transformer on fire.
Lesson Learned: We overhauled the fire suppression strategy for oil transformers. Standard water deluge is banned. We installed High-Velocity Water Spray (HVWS) nozzles that create a fine, emulsifying mist that cools the oil surface without penetrating it, smothering the fire instantly. Never fight an oil fire with a straight hose stream.
