A contractor entered a caster maintenance platform through an unlocked gate while the adjacent strand was operating. The platform looked identical to the isolated strand’s platform, and a temporary sign had blown down.

The spotter challenged the person before he reached the danger zone. No injury occurred. The investigation found that isolation boundaries were marked on a drawing but not on the floor, both access gates used similar labels, and the contractor’s induction had covered general caster hazards without a physical walkdown.

Actions included locking unused gates, adding high-visibility strand identification, restoring signs with secondary fixing, and requiring a joint field verification before platform access. The permit form was changed to record the exact strand and access point.

Lesson: people follow the path that looks available. Do not rely on a small sign or a screen label when adjacent equipment is identical. Physical boundary, locked access, and a field walkdown are stronger than paperwork alone.
