Scenario: A direct lightning strike to the 120-meter Sinter Plant main exhaust stack during a severe summer thunderstorm.
Action Taken: The physical structure of the stack handled the strike fine via its lightning rods, but the massive electromagnetic pulse (EMP) induced a voltage spike into the instrument cabling running down the side of the stack. This spike bypassed the standard surge protectors and completely wiped the EPROM memory of the main exhaust fan PLC cards in the control room. The fan tripped instantly, choking the plant.
Lesson Learned: Standard grounding is not enough for EMP induction. We had to replace all copper signal cables on the external stack with fiber-optic lines, which are immune to electromagnetic interference, preventing a repeat of this total control loss.
