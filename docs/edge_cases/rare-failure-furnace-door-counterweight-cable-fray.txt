A furnace-door counterweight cable showed broken wires during a routine round. Door operation was stopped and the counterweight secured. The cable was replaced and sheaves inspected for sharp edges. Record door, cable, load, broken wires, sheave, and isolation. A cable can fail suddenly even when the door still moves. Do not stand beneath a counterweight or continue until the full system is checked.  The event was added to handover because it may recur under similar material, weather, load, or maintenance conditions. Protect people first, preserve evidence, and identify the early sign, safe boundary, escalation point, and proof required before normal operation. Do not call an unusual alarm harmless merely because the plant recovered once. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
