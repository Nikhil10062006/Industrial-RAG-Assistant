Scenario: Charging liquid iron into a Torpedo Car that had been parked outside, empty, with its lid open during a severe monsoon downpour.
The car was lined with refractory bricks, which are porous. The bricks absorbed roughly 50 liters of rainwater. The shift operator failed to inspect the car and signaled the blast furnace to tap 300 tons of 1500°C liquid iron into it.
Action Taken: When the liquid iron hit the water-soaked bricks, the water flashed instantly to steam. The volumetric expansion of steam caused a massive explosion, blasting a fountain of liquid iron 20 meters into the air, melting the cast-house roof panels and severing the main crane power cables.
Lesson Learned: If an empty hot metal vessel of any kind (ladle, torpedo, slag pot) is exposed to rain, it is immediately quarantined. It must be moved to the gas pre-heater stands and baked at 800°C for a minimum of 12 hours to drive out all structural moisture before a single drop of hot metal touches it.
