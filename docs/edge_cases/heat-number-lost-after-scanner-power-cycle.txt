A slab identification scanner lost its heat number after a brief power interruption. The operator manually re-entered the number from memory, but the production sequence showed a different grade. The slab was stopped before entering the rolling line.

The scanner database had retained the image but not the association because the transaction had not been committed. The manual fallback screen allowed entry without requiring a second-person check or certificate match.

The slab was quarantined, and the heat number was verified against ladle records, caster sequence, and the physical mark. The scanner UPS was checked, the transaction logic was corrected, and a power-cycle test was added to commissioning checks.

Lesson: after a power or network interruption, never trust the last visible identity. Verify physical mark, heat record, grade, width, and sequence. A barcode or OCR system is an aid; the material identity remains a quality-critical control.
