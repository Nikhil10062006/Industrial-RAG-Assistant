During intense rain, a wastewater sump pump ran continuously but delivered little flow. The sump level fell below the suction because stormwater had been diverted to another path, while the level switch was stuck high by debris. The pump was stopped and the impeller inspected for damage. The switch was cleaned, the suction condition restored, and the stormwater route reviewed. Record rainfall, sump level, switch state, pump current, discharge pressure, and dry-run duration. Veteran note: high rain does not guarantee high level at every sump. Check actual level, float freedom, suction submergence, and cross-connections. A pump can be damaged by running dry even during a storm.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
