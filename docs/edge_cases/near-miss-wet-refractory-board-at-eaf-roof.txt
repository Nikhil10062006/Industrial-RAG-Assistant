A refractory board stored under a leaking roof was selected for an EAF roof repair. The board looked dry on the top face but was damp underneath. A technician noticed the weight and rejected it before installation. No injury occurred. The material was quarantined and the storage area inspected. The near miss showed that packaging and roof checks were not linked to refractory issue. Update the store inspection to include underside moisture, batch identity, and shelf condition. Do not dry questionable refractory with an improvised heater. Record material, batch, location, weather, and disposition. Lesson: refractory dryness is part of process safety. A wet board near molten steel can release steam or spall violently. Material control begins in the store, not at the furnace.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
