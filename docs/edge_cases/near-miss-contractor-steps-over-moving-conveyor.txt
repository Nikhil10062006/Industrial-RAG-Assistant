A contractor stepped over a slow-moving conveyor because the designated walkway was blocked by a hose. The operator stopped the belt after seeing the movement. No injury occurred. The investigation found poor housekeeping, unclear route marking, and pressure to reach a work area before a permit briefing. The hose was rerouted, the walkway restored, and the task briefing changed to include the route. Record conveyor, location, obstruction, contractor, and controls. Lesson: speed and familiarity create shortcuts. A moving belt remains dangerous at slow speed, and stepping over it is not acceptable. Access routes must be protected as part of the job plan, not left to the worker to improvise.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
