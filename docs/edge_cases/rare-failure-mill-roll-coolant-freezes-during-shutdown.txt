During an unusually cold shutdown, residual water in a roll-coolant branch froze and split a small hose. The leak appeared only when the header was repressurized on the next shift. The line was isolated and the affected stand held while hoses, nozzles, and drain points were inspected. The branch was drained and protected before restart. Record ambient temperature, shutdown duration, coolant composition, low points, hose damage, and pressure test. Veteran note: a closed valve does not remove water trapped between the valve and nozzle. Seasonal shutdown checks must include drains, heat tracing, insulation, and low points. Do not pressurize a suspected frozen branch; thaw and inspect it under the approved method.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
