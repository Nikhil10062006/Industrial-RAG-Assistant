A maintenance team nearly connected an oxygen cylinder to a fuel-gas burner because the cylinders were stored together and the hose fittings had been adapted during a temporary job. The operator noticed the label mismatch before opening the valve. The cylinders were separated, adapters removed, and the burner connection standardized. Record cylinder numbers, service, fitting type, storage arrangement, and corrective action. Lesson: color and shape are not enough; read labels, use keyed fittings, and control temporary adapters. Oxygen connection to a fuel system can create a severe fire. The near miss was used in contractor induction and workshop inspection.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
