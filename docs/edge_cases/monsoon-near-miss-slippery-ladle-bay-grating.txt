During monsoon, water entered the ladle bay and made grating slippery near a transfer route. A worker slipped but caught the handrail before falling toward the hot-metal path. The area was barricaded, water removed, and drainage checked. The source was a damaged roof gutter rather than process water. Record location, weather, route, footwear, drainage, and corrective action. Lesson: ordinary rainwater becomes a hot-metal hazard when it changes footing and escape speed. Keep routes dry, repair roof drainage, and stop movement when the walking surface is unsafe. Heat PPE does not guarantee traction.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
