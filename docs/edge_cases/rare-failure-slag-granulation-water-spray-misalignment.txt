Slag granulation water spray began striking the chute wall instead of the stream, creating steam and irregular granule size. The pump pressure was normal, but a nozzle bracket had shifted after vibration. The system was placed in the approved safe condition, the nozzle alignment corrected, and the chute inspected for erosion. Record water pressure, nozzle angle, steam, granule condition, and bracket integrity. Lesson: a spray system can have normal pressure and poor process coverage. Never approach a hot granulation chute to adjust a nozzle while water or slag is flowing. Repeated bracket movement may require a better support or locking method.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
