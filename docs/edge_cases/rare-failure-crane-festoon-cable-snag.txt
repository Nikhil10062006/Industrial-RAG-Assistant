A crane festoon cable snagged on a protruding bracket during long travel. The cable did not fail, but movement stopped and the load remained safely suspended. The crane was isolated, the bracket removed or guarded, and the cable inspected. Record crane, travel direction, cable, snag point, and load. A snag can pull control conductors or damage insulation. Walk the route after structural work and test unloaded before ladle service.  The event was added to handover because it may recur under similar material, weather, load, or maintenance conditions. Protect people first, preserve evidence, and identify the early sign, safe boundary, escalation point, and proof required before normal operation. Do not call an unusual alarm harmless merely because the plant recovered once. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
