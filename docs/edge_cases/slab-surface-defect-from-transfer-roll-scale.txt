A repeating line defect appeared on slabs only when the transfer table was used after a long idle period. The caster strand surface was clean and the defect pitch matched one table roll circumference.

The transfer roll had accumulated a hard scale ridge while cooling water was off. The ridge contacted the slab during the first transfers. Normal operation later wore the ridge down, which explained why the defect disappeared.

The roll was inspected during the next gap, cleaned, and checked for damage. A slow dry rotation check was added after long idle periods. Quality was notified to hold slabs made during the affected window.

Lesson: recurring pitch is a useful clue. Measure the defect spacing and compare it with roll circumference, not only with caster or mould length. A defect that disappears after several pieces may still have affected the first pieces.

The affected slabs were traced to the exact idle-period window and held for quality review. The roll surface was checked again after the first hot transfer. A dry rotation check is now required after long cooling-water outages because the first piece can carry the defect before normal water flow returns.
