A worker began opening a manual steam valve without confirming downstream equipment was ready. Another operator stopped the action after noticing the line was cold and full of condensate. The warm-up procedure was followed and drain points opened. Record valve, line, downstream user, condition, and action. Lesson: a valve position is a process action. Check pressure, temperature, drains, and people before moving it.  The event was discussed with the next shift and converted into a physical or procedural control where possible. The lesson is not “be careful”; it is to remove the confusing label, blocked route, missing barrier, wrong tool, or uncontrolled assumption that made the near miss possible. Keep the affected equipment and records traceable. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
