During a preheat inspection, a crack was found at a tundish-cover lifting lug. The cover had been lifted several times without incident and the crack was hidden beneath scale. The cover was quarantined, the crane route held, and dye inspection confirmed the defect. The lug was repaired or the cover replaced according to engineering disposition. Record cover number, lug location, lifting history, inspection method, and repair. Lesson: repeated thermal cycling can weaken lifting points even when the cover itself looks sound. Do not lift by an unapproved edge or use a nearby hole as a substitute lug. Inspect lugs, welds, pins, and balance before every lift. The lifting plan must define what happens if one lug is rejected.

The condition was added to the shift handover because it may recur under similar weather, load, material, or maintenance conditions. The immediate response protected people first, then preserved evidence for the investigation. Do not treat an unusual event as harmless because the plant recovered. Check adjacent equipment, temporary repairs, alarms, and records from the affected window. The practical lesson is to identify the early sign, the safe boundary, the escalation point, and the evidence required before normal operation is restored.
