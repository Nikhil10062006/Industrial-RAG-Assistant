A new isolation valve on a caster cooling branch caused a sharp bang and pipe movement when opened. No leak appeared, but the pressure transmitter spiked above its normal range.

The valve had a fast quarter-turn actuator and the control logic commanded full open immediately. The branch also contained trapped air after maintenance. The combination produced water hammer.

The branch was isolated and inspected for support damage. Air was vented at the designated point, the actuator opening time was adjusted, and the pressure trend was reviewed during a slow refill. The valve was not returned to automatic until the mechanical supports were checked.

Lesson: a valve can be correctly installed and still be wrong for the system dynamics. After piping work, fill slowly, vent air, watch supports, and test the control stroke. A single bang can loosen a flange or damage a coil inside the equipment.
