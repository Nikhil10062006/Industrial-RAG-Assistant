Scenario: A severe "hearth chill" at the Blast Furnace after a prolonged, unplanned 4-day shutdown. The molten iron and slag in the hearth had frozen solid, blocking the tapholes.
Action Taken: You cannot push hot blast wind into a frozen hearth; it will channel up the walls and burn the copper staves. We had to melt the hearth from the bottom up using oxygen lancing. We rigged heavy-duty 1-inch oxygen lances and literally burned our way through the frozen tapholes. We then injected raw oxygen directly into the hearth through the tuyeres to violently burn the dead coke and generate localized extreme heat to melt the frozen iron. It took 72 hours of continuous, grueling, high-hazard lancing to re-establish liquid flow.
Lesson Learned: Never let the furnace sit idle for more than 48 hours without tapping the hearth completely dry. If a strike or blackout forces a long delay, you must drain the furnace to the absolute lowest level before the iron freezes.
