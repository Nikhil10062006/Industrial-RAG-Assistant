A newly patched ladle lining spalled during preheat and small pieces fell into the bottom. The patch had passed the visual inspection and the burner flame was normal.

The repair compound had not reached the required dry-out schedule because the ladle was needed earlier than planned. A rapid heat-up drove moisture and binder gases through the patch. The outer surface looked dry, which led the team to continue.

Preheating was stopped, the ladle was isolated and cooled according to the refractory instruction, and the patch was removed and rebuilt. The dry-out curve was added to the ladle status board, including actual hold temperatures and times. The ladle was not released based only on the final surface appearance.

Lesson: refractory history matters. Ask when the patch was made, how it was dried, whether rain or wash water entered, and whether the burner profile was followed. A patch that spalls into the ladle can damage the slide gate, contaminate steel, or create a flow restriction.
