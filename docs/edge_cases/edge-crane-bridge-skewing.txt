Scenario: The massive 400-ton Ladle Crane began to "crab" or skew diagonally while traveling down the runway, causing the wheel flanges to aggressively grind and climb the rails, popping the rail clips off the girders.
Action Taken: The mechanical team assumed the bridge motors were out of sync. We checked the drive VFDs, and they were perfectly matched. The real root cause was asymmetrical wear on the tapered crane wheels. The wheels on the drive side had worn down 5mm smaller in diameter than the idler side. A smaller wheel travels a shorter distance per revolution. Every time the crane drove forward, the drive side lagged behind, twisting the massive bridge structure out of square.
Lesson Learned: You cannot replace crane wheels individually. If one wheel wears out, you must replace the wheels on both sides of the bridge as a perfectly matched, precision-machined pair.
