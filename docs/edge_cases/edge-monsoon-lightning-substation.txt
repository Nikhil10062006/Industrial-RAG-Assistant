Scenario: Massive monsoonal flooding entering the underground cable trenches of the main 132kV/33kV plant receiving substation.
Action Taken: The drainage pumps in the cable cellar tripped due to a lightning strike that blew out their local control panel. Water rapidly filled the concrete trenches housing the main 33kV incoming feeder cables. While the cables are insulated, the splices and joints are vulnerable. If water breaches a 33kV joint, it will cause a phase-to-ground fault that will blow a crater in the floor and trip the entire integrated steel plant.
We mobilized four diesel-driven trash pumps from the fire department. We had to break open the concrete trench covers in the yard and pump the water directly into the storm drains, bypassing the cellar entirely.
Lesson Learned: Never route the power supply for the trench drainage pumps through the same substation they are protecting. They must be fed from a separate, independent emergency UPS or generator circuit so a substation fault doesn't also kill the very pumps meant to save it.
