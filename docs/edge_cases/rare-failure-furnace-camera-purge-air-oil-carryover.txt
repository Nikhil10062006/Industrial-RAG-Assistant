A furnace camera window became coated after compressor oil carryover entered the purge air. The image slowly darkened and operators increased furnace adjustments unnecessarily. The purge source was isolated, filter and separator serviced, and the camera cleaned. Record purge pressure, oil condition, image quality, and compressor state. Clean air quality protects optics and process observation. A dark image should trigger purge inspection before camera replacement.  The event was added to handover because it may recur under similar material, weather, load, or maintenance conditions. Protect people first, preserve evidence, and identify the early sign, safe boundary, escalation point, and proof required before normal operation. Do not call an unusual alarm harmless merely because the plant recovered once. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
