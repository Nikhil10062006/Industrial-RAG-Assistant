A dust-suppression line at a sinter transfer point had no spray during a cold early shift. The pump was running and header pressure was normal upstream.

The nozzle branch had trapped water and froze after the line was left full overnight. Operators increased pump pressure, which caused a hose to bulge at a weak connection. The hose was isolated before it failed.

The branch was drained after shutdown, the nozzle was replaced, and a low-point drain and heat trace were added. The pump pressure was returned to normal. Dust control was restored before full material flow resumed.

Lesson: a pressure reading upstream does not prove every branch is open. In cold conditions, drain low points and check exposed hoses. Never exceed hose or nozzle pressure to force a frozen branch open.

The branch was marked with its drain point and winter line-up. Operators were told to confirm spray at the transfer before starting full feed, not after dust becomes visible. A frozen nozzle is a branch condition; raising the common header pressure is not an approved thawing method.
