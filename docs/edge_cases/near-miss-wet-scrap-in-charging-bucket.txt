A scrap bucket was lifted toward the furnace when the crane operator noticed water draining from the bottom. The bucket was lowered into the inspection area and the charge was rejected. The scrap had been stored under a leaking roof, and a hollow section was holding water inside. The outside looked only damp.

No person was injured, but the potential outcome was severe. Water trapped under scrap can flash to steam when it contacts molten metal and eject metal or slag from the furnace. The near miss was not caused by the crane or furnace interlock; it was a material-control failure.

Actions taken were straightforward. The scrap supplier and yard team were informed. The bucket inspection was changed to include tilting or draining only at the approved safe point. Hollow sections, sealed drums, wet turnings, and material from flood-affected areas were added to the rejection examples. During the monsoon, the yard supervisor must verify roof drainage and cover integrity each shift.

The lesson for new operators is simple: “dry-looking” is not the same as dry. Do not shake or probe a suspicious bucket over the furnace. Stop, lower it safely, isolate the area, and ask the scrap controller to decide the disposition.
