Scenario: Catastrophic burn-through of a copper tuyere at the Blast Furnace, followed by a failure of the automatic water shutoff.
A massive chunk of semi-solid iron fell from the furnace wall and landed directly on Tuyere 8, melting the nose of the copper cooler instantly. The leak was so severe that cooling water started pouring directly into the 1500°C hearth at a rate of 200 liters per minute. The water dissociated into hydrogen and oxygen gas—a highly explosive mix.
Action Taken: The shift manager hit the "Emergency Blast Cut" button. We slashed the hot blast wind to zero to kill the furnace reaction and immediately opened the nitrogen purge valves to flood the furnace with inert gas, suppressing the hydrogen explosion risk. The mechanical team had to don aluminized suits and manually isolate the massive water supply valves on the bustle ring, as the pneumatic actuators had melted in the heat.
Lesson Learned: A burned tuyere is bad; a water-filled hearth is a bomb. If the delta-flow alarms show a leak >50 L/min, do not hesitate to cut the blast. You can replace a tuyere in 2 hours. If the hearth explodes, the plant is down for a year.
