A coil was tight on one side and loose on the other after recoiling. Mandrel pressure and expansion command were normal.

The hydraulic expansion cylinder was healthy. One mandrel segment had a worn guide and did not travel fully, leaving a smaller radius on the drive side. The position sensor reported average cylinder travel and did not detect the individual segment problem.

The coil was placed on hold, the mandrel was isolated and inspected, and the worn guide was replaced. A mechanical pattern check was added to the mandrel inspection, not just a pressure check.

Lesson: average position feedback can hide uneven mechanical movement. If coil telescoping follows one side, inspect mandrel segments, keys, guides, and chocks before changing tension. A good pressure number does not guarantee a good coil shape.

The mandrel was cycled without strip after repair, and each segment position was checked against the mechanical reference. A trial coil was inspected for tightness, telescoping, and edge damage before full production. The defect record now requires the affected side and segment number where known.
