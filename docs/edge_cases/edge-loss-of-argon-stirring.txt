Scenario: Complete failure of the argon bottom-stirring plug in a 300-ton ladle of liquid steel at the Ladle Furnace.
Argon gas is bubbled through the bottom of the ladle to stir the steel, homogenize the temperature, and float out impurities. The porous refractory plug in the bottom of the ladle became completely choked with sintered metal. Without stirring, the top of the ladle got superheated by the electrodes, while the bottom remained cold. If sent to the caster like this, the cold bottom would freeze in the slide gate, causing a catastrophic abort.
Action Taken: We couldn't abort a 300-ton heat. We brought in the emergency top-lance. We lowered a refractory-coated steel pipe from the crane directly down through the slag into the deep bath and injected argon from the top. It's violent, messy, and damages the ladle lining, but it forced enough mixing to equalize the temperature.
Lesson Learned: If bottom flow drops below 50 L/min, stop heating immediately. You are just cooking the slag and destroying the upper refractory. Deploy the top lance before the thermal stratification gets too severe.
