Two sample cups from adjacent heats were placed on the same tray. A laboratory technician noticed the time sequence did not match the heat cards and stopped analysis. The samples were separated and identity rebuilt from operator notes and heat timestamps. The tray was relabeled and one-sample-at-a-time handling restored. Record heat numbers, sample times, people involved, and corrective action. Lesson: similar-looking samples need physical segregation and immediate labeling. Never guess identity because chemistry is plausible.  The event was discussed with the next shift and converted into a physical or procedural control where possible. The lesson is not “be careful”; it is to remove the confusing label, blocked route, missing barrier, wrong tool, or uncontrolled assumption that made the near miss possible. Keep the affected equipment and records traceable. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
