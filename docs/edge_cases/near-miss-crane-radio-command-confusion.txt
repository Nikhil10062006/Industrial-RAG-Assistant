Two crane operators on the same radio channel heard a command to “move left” and one crane started to travel. The signalman intended the other crane. The operator stopped when the load alarm sounded; no collision occurred.

The cause was identical crane call signs, poor location wording, and one signalman talking while another person repeated the command. The channel had no formal command structure in that bay.

The area introduced unique call signs, mandatory location and direction in every movement command, and one designated signalman per crane. Operators must repeat critical commands before moving. A crane that receives an unclear or conflicting command remains stopped.

Lesson: radio discipline is an engineering control for suspended loads. “Left” has no meaning without crane identity, bay, and travel direction. If two cranes share a channel, clarity must be designed, not expected.

The revised call uses crane identifier, bay, direction, distance, and action, followed by read-back. Signalmen are not permitted to issue commands to both cranes at once. The incident was discussed with contractors because a new operator may not know which voices are authorized.
