A shell-temperature alarm appeared only during a speed ramp, while mould level and water flow were stable. Trend review showed the alarm followed a sensor threshold and a temporary spray change rather than a thermal runaway. The speed was held, companion sensors checked, and the shell condition reviewed under the approved response. Record speed, sensor quality, water flow, mould level, and alarm timing. A false positive is possible, but no breakout monitor is dismissed without evidence. Use the alarm sequence to distinguish signal quality from process movement.  The event was added to handover because it may recur under similar material, weather, load, or maintenance conditions. Protect people first, preserve evidence, and identify the early sign, safe boundary, escalation point, and proof required before normal operation. Do not call an unusual alarm harmless merely because the plant recovered once. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
