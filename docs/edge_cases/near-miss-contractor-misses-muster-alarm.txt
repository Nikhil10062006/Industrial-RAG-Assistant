A contractor did not hear the muster alarm because hearing protection and a running grinder masked the signal. A buddy noticed the absence during roll call. The work group was added to the radio notification and visual beacon review. Record area, task, alarm, communication, and action. Lesson: emergency notification must reach noisy and enclosed work areas. Attendance lists must include contractors and visitors.  The event was discussed with the next shift and converted into a physical or procedural control where possible. The lesson is not “be careful”; it is to remove the confusing label, blocked route, missing barrier, wrong tool, or uncontrolled assumption that made the near miss possible. Keep the affected equipment and records traceable. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
