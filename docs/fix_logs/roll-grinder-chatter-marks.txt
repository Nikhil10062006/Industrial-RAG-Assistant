A ground roll showed repeating chatter marks and transferred them to the strip. Wheel balance and spindle bearings were checked; the coolant nozzle was misdirected and wheel loading was high. The roll was reground after correcting coolant and dressing settings. Record wheel, speed, feed, coolant flow, roll material, and surface result. Do not polish over chatter without removing the pattern. Compare mark pitch with wheel or spindle speed to locate the source.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
