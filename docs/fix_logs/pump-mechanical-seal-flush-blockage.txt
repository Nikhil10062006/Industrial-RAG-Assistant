Symptom: a process pump seal leaked after a new seal was installed. Seal flush pressure was below the required differential and the flush filter contained scale. The seal faces had run hot and were damaged. The pump was isolated, the flush line cleaned, and the seal replaced after checking shaft runout and alignment. Record seal type, flush pressure, process pressure, filter condition, leakage, and alignment. Veteran note: a new seal cannot survive a blocked flush. Check the seal plan, valve lineup, orifice, filter, and source pressure before blaming installation. Do not open a seal pot or flush line without isolating pressure. After restart, watch leakage during warm-up and at operating temperature; a seal that is dry for five minutes is not yet proven.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
