Symptom: a hydraulic cylinder began leaking at the rod seal and movement became jerky. The rod had longitudinal scoring caused by a damaged wiper and embedded scale. Pressure was normal, so the leak was initially treated as a seal-only problem. The cylinder was isolated, removed under the lifting plan, and the rod measured for repair limits. The wiper, seal set, and contaminated oil were replaced, and the guide was checked for side load. Record cylinder tag, pressure, rod condition, leakage rate, alignment, and oil cleanliness. Veteran note: replacing a seal on a scored rod gives a short repair and a repeat leak. Look for chips, side loading, misaligned mounts, and poor wiper condition. Never wipe a pressurized rod by hand. After installation, bleed air safely and test through the full stroke at low pressure before production.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
