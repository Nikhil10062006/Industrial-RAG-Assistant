Problem: Coke Oven Battery 5, Oven #42 pusher-side door caught fire 10 minutes after being charged with fresh coal. Flames were shooting 3 meters up the side of the buckstays.
Root Cause: The door cleaner mechanism was inspected and found to have done a perfect job; the knife-edge seal on the door was clean. The problem wasn't mechanical dirt. The problem was battery pressure. The main exhausters were pulling too hard, putting the battery collecting main under a slight negative pressure (-2 mmWC) instead of the required positive pressure (+10 mmWC). The negative pressure sucked outside air straight through the microscopic gaps in the door seal, burning the tar that was supposed to condense and plug those gaps. No tar seal meant raw coke oven gas leaked out and ignited.
Fix: Radioed the By-Products control room to adjust the Askania regulating valve on the collecting main to restore positive pressure to +12 mmWC. Sprayed the leaking door with the clay-luting slurry gun to choke the fire.
Pro-Tip: A clean door will still leak and burn if the battery pressure is negative. Fix the pressure before you blame the door seals.
