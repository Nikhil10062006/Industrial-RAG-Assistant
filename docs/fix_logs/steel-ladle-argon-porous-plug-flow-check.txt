Symptom: argon stirring was weak and uneven during ladle treatment. Gas pressure at the station was normal, but the flow reading was low. The porous-plug line was checked for valve position, hose condition, and leakage. A partly blocked regulator filter and a damaged quick coupling were found. After isolation and replacement, flow returned to the approved range, but the plug response remained uneven because slag had covered part of the plug face. The treatment was completed under the steelmaking engineer’s direction with adjusted stirring time. Veteran note: a normal header pressure does not prove useful flow through the plug. Compare pressure, flow, bubble behavior, and slag movement. A sudden pressure rise with falling flow suggests restriction; low pressure with unexpected bubbling outside the ladle suggests a leak. Record gas pressure, flow, treatment time, plug identity, slag condition, and any deviation in temperature or chemistry.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
