A hydraulic return-filter differential alarm appeared after a cylinder repair. The filter contained seal fragments from the old cylinder and the oil temperature was normal. The unit was placed in the safe condition, filter replaced, and oil sampled. Record differential pressure, oil temperature, filter contents, repair history, and sample result. Do not bypass a return filter to keep production moving; contamination can damage every servo valve downstream.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
