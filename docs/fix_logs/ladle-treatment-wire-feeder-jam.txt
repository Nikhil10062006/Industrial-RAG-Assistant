Wire feed stopped with motor current high and the guide tube vibrating. The wire had a flattened section from a poor coil payout. The feeder was stopped, tension released, and the wire cut back to sound material. Roll pressure and guide alignment were checked before restart. Record wire type, speed, current, guide condition, and coil batch. Do not pull a stuck wire by hand while the feeder is energized. A small flattening can create a jam several metres downstream.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
