An equalization-tank mixer stopped and solids began settling. Motor current was zero because a coupling insert had failed. The tank was managed to prevent overflow, the mixer isolated, and the coupling replaced. Record mixer, level, current, torque, solids, and time stopped. A motor run signal is not proof of mixing. Prolonged settling can change downstream chemistry and sludge loading.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
