Coating gauge showed a gradual bias against laboratory measurement. The sensor window had zinc dust and the reference standard was out of date. The gauge was removed from automatic control, cleaned, and checked with a valid standard. Affected coils were held for review. Record gauge, standard, as-found bias, cleaning, strip speed, and coating result. A smooth bias is often reference or window condition, not random electronics. Do not change bath settings based on an unverified gauge.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
