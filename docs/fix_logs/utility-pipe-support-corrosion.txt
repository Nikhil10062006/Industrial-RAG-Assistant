A steam pipe support showed corrosion and sagging near a drain. The pipe was still in service, but movement had changed and insulation was wet. The section was assessed under the piping integrity process, load reduced if required, and support repaired during a controlled outage. Record pipe, support, temperature, corrosion, insulation, and movement. Do not weld or jack a hot loaded pipe without engineering approval. Small support defects can become flange leaks or pipe rupture.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
