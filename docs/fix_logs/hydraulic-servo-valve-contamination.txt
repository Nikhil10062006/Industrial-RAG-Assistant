Operating area: Mechanical, electrical, automation, and workshop maintenance. Start with safe isolation, equipment identity, stored energy, and a preserved fault history. Obtain field evidence before changing parts or logic. A servo valve became sluggish after dirty oil passed a damaged filter seal. Sample oil, inspect filter, valve spool, contamination, pressure, and command response. The technician recorded the as-found condition before the repair and did not hide the first symptom. Record the equipment tag, material or heat identity, time, readings with units, people informed, and the final status. If a temporary control, bypass, manual setting, or alternate route is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not close the event because the alarm cleared or the equipment restarted once. Repeat the relevant test under the same load, speed, temperature, pressure, or material condition that caused the problem. At handover, state what was checked, what remains uncertain, and the exact condition that requires stopping the equipment. Practical plant knowledge is useful when it makes the next safe decision easier without replacing isolation, permit, quality, emergency, or engineering controls.
