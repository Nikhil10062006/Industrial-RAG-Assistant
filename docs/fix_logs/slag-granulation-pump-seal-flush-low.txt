A granulation pump seal began leaking while seal-flush pressure fell. The flush filter was blocked with fine slag and the source valve was only partly open. The pump was changed over, the filter cleaned, and the seal inspected. Record pump pressure, flush pressure, water temperature, leakage, and filter condition. A seal leak near hot slag requires a controlled shutdown and exclusion zone. Do not use a hand to locate a high-pressure water jet.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
