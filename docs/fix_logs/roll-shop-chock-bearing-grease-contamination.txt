Symptom: a work-roll chock bearing temperature increased after routine greasing. Inspection found water and fine scale in the grease cavity; the grease gun coupling had not sealed cleanly and the cap was missing. The chock was removed, cleaned, inspected, and repacked with the specified grease. The seal and cap were replaced. Record bearing temperature, grease type, amount, water presence, seal condition, and roll assignment. Veteran note: grease that looks full can still be contaminated. Milky or gritty grease is a condition report, not an invitation to add more. Clean the nipple before connection and keep grease equipment capped. If temperature rises after lubrication, compare quantity, viscosity, seal drag, and bearing condition before declaring the grease wrong.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
