A hot-saw blade lost a tooth during billet cutting. The saw was stopped and the blade area isolated. Inspection found a hard inclusion in the billet and a damaged coolant nozzle, causing local overheating. The blade was replaced, coolant pattern restored, and cut samples inspected. Record billet grade, speed, blade ID, coolant flow, tooth condition, and fragments. Keep people outside the blade plane during coast-down. A tooth break can damage guards and send fragments into the area.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
