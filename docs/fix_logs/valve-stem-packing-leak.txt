Symptom: a steam or water valve showed leakage at the stem packing while the body and flange were intact. The packing gland had been tightened unevenly and the stem had corrosion marks. The valve was isolated and cooled or depressurized under the line procedure. Packing was renewed with the correct material and the stem surface inspected. The gland was tightened evenly and tested through several open-close cycles. Record service, pressure, temperature, packing type, leak rate, stem condition, and final test. Veteran note: tightening a hot packing gland blindly can seize the stem or cause a sudden release. A packing leak may also indicate vibration, poor alignment, wrong packing, or a damaged stem. Do not approach a steam jet with a hand. If the valve cannot isolate, use the positive-isolation plan before repair.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
