A coil car would not release its brake after the command. Air pressure was normal but the brake solenoid was contaminated with scale. The car remained chocked, power isolated, and the solenoid cleaned and tested. Record car, pressure, brake state, load, rail condition, and test. Do not tow a braked car with a crane or vehicle unless the recovery plan permits. A stuck brake can damage wheels and rails; a released brake can roll unexpectedly.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
