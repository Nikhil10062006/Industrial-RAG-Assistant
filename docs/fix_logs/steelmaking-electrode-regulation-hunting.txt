Symptom: electrode regulation began hunting during melting, with current fluctuating and electrode movement more frequent than normal. Hydraulic pressure was stable. The position feedback from one mast was noisy because the cable carrier had damaged shielding near a high-current conductor. The control system interpreted the noise as rapid arc-length changes. The furnace was stabilized under the approved operating mode and the affected signal was compared with local position and neighboring electrode behavior. The cable was repaired, shielding restored, and the feedback loop tested through a controlled movement. The regulation gain was returned to the standard value; it was not left reduced to mask the problem. Tribal knowledge: if one electrode hunts while the others remain steady and hydraulic pressure is normal, inspect signal quality and cable routing before changing furnace practice. Record current, voltage, electrode position, hydraulic pressure, signal quality, alarm sequence, and arc stability. High-current equipment can create intermittent faults that disappear when the furnace stops, so preserve the trend before resetting.
