One bell-cooling return temperature rose while flow remained normal. Inspection found a partially blocked return passage and scale at the temperature pocket. The circuit was managed under the furnace cooling procedure, cleaned during the safe window, and tested against neighboring circuits. Record inlet flow, inlet and outlet temperature, pressure, and recent water-quality results. A normal flow switch cannot prove adequate heat removal if the return path is restricted. Do not increase pump pressure blindly; confirm the circuit and strainer first.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
