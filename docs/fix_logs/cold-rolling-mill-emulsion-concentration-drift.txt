Symptom: emulsion concentration fell below target and strip temperature rose. The mixing tank level and pump pressure were normal. The concentrate dosing pump was running, but its suction foot valve leaked and air entered the line. The lab sample was taken from a stagnant branch and initially understated the variation. A representative sample was collected from the circulating line, the pump was repaired, and the emulsion was adjusted according to the process instruction. Filters and nozzles were inspected for the effect of low concentration. Record concentration, pH, temperature, tank level, dosing rate, sample point, and strip temperature. Veteran note: a running dosing pump may be moving air. Check stroke, suction pressure, discharge pulse, and foot valve condition. Do not add concentrate based on one poor sample without confirming location and circulation; overcorrection can create foaming and residue.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
