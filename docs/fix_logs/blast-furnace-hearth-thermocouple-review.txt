Symptom: one hearth thermocouple climbed steadily while neighboring points remained normal. The control room first suspected a bad sensor because the furnace process variables had not changed. Field inspection found a damaged protection tube near a refractory repair, allowing the thermocouple to see a local hot spot. The sensor was isolated under the approved instrument procedure and compared with a calibrated spare and shell-temperature trend. The thermocouple was replaced, but the location was not dismissed as an instrument-only issue. Engineering reviewed the hearth cooling-water flow and the repair history before the furnace returned to normal operation. The reading fell to the expected band after replacement, with no other evidence of localized heating. Tribal knowledge: a single high hearth temperature can be a sensor problem, but the consequence of assuming that too early is serious. Check sensor installation, cable integrity, cooling, and nearby process indications. Record as-found value, neighboring values, process rate, repair history, and the exact decision to continue or reduce operation.
