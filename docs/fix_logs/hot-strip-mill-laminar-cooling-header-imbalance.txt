Symptom: exit temperature varied across strip width even though total laminar-cooling flow was within target. Thermal images showed the drive side was cooler. Inspection found a partially closed header valve and a blocked filter on the opposite bank. After isolation, the filter was cleaned, valve position restored, and spray pattern verified using the approved test. The first coil was held for temperature review. Veteran note: total water flow can be correct while one width zone is wrong. Compare header pressures, individual bank flows, spray fan shape, and strip temperature profile. Do not increase the full pump setpoint to cure a local restriction; this can overcool other areas and overload the line. Record coil width, speed, entry temperature, each bank’s pressure and flow, filter condition, and final profile. If the imbalance follows a valve position or maintenance event, check the physical lineup before changing the cooling model.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
