Problem: The 4 MW centrifugal main plant air compressor began emitting a terrifying, rhythmic roaring noise every 3 seconds, shaking the entire compressor house foundation.
Root Cause: The compressor had entered an aerodynamic "surge." The steel plant's demand for air had dropped significantly because the Melt Shop went on a maintenance delay. The compressor's control system tried to maintain the 7-bar setpoint by closing the Inlet Guide Vanes (IGVs). However, the automated anti-surge blow-off valve (which is supposed to vent excess air to atmosphere to keep minimum flow through the impellers) was mechanically stuck closed due to a rusted pneumatic actuator stem. With nowhere for the air to go, the high-pressure air in the pipeline forcefully reversed direction and flowed backward through the compressor every few seconds.
Fix: Immediately hit the manual E-stop on the compressor. Forcing it to run in surge for even a minute will shatter the impellers. Replaced the actuator on the blow-off valve.
Pro-Tip: A surging centrifugal compressor sounds like a jet engine coughing. Don't troubleshoot the control loop while it's surging—trip it immediately.
