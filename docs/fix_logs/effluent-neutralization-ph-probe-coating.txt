Symptom: neutralization pH control began oscillating and lab samples disagreed with the online probe. Inspection found coating on the probe and a blocked reference junction. The dosing pumps were healthy. The system was placed in the approved manual or controlled mode while the probe was removed, cleaned, and checked against buffers. The sample point was flushed and compared with a representative laboratory sample. Record pH before and after cleaning, temperature, tank mixing, dosing rate, probe condition, and calibration slope. Veteran note: a pH probe can drift slowly and still show a smooth trend. Check mixing and sample location as well as calibration. Do not dose aggressively to chase a coated probe; this can create a real chemical excursion. Online instruments in wastewater require cleaning frequency based on fouling, not only calendar time.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
