Stand force was consistently high on one product family after a roll change. Gap zero was correct, but the roll diameter entered in the setup was 4 mm smaller than the physical card. The value was corrected, unloaded force checked, and the first coils held for thickness review. Record stand, roll number, diameter, force, gap, and setup revision. A plausible setup error can produce stable but wrong reduction. Always compare the screen with the roll card after a change.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
