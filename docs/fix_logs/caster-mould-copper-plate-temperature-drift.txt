Symptom: one mould-copper temperature channel drifted upward slowly while neighbouring channels stayed stable. Water flow and mould level were normal. Field inspection found the thermocouple tip had moved away from the intended copper location after a retaining clip loosened. The sensor was not actually seeing the hot zone it was meant to monitor. The strand was managed within the caster engineer’s approved limits while the sensor was isolated and replaced. The clip and protection arrangement were corrected, and the channel was checked against a reference. Tribal knowledge: a stable temperature trend can still be wrong if the sensor has shifted. Compare channel patterns, water temperature, mould width, and strip or strand behavior. Do not change cooling or speed based on one unverified channel. Record sensor position, as-found reading, neighboring values, heat number, and acceptance test. Mechanical retention is part of instrument reliability in a vibrating, hot caster environment.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
