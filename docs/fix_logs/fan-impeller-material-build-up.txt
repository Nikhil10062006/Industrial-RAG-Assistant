Symptom: induced-draft fan vibration rose gradually with no bearing temperature increase. Inspection found dust and sticky material deposited unevenly on the impeller. The fan was isolated, cleaned, and inspected for erosion and cracks. Balance was checked before restart. Record fan speed, vibration direction, current, dust condition, filter differential pressure, and impeller inspection. Veteran note: a fan can be mechanically sound and still become unbalanced through buildup. Do not change bearing or coupling first if the vibration follows process dust loading. Cleaning must control stored energy, dust exposure, and fall hazards. Check the source of moisture or sticky material if buildup returns quickly.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
