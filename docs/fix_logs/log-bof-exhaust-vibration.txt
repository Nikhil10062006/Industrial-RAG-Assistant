Problem: The massive 6 MW BOF Primary Exhaust ID Fan started tripping on high vibration (over 7 mm/s) exclusively during the middle of the oxygen blow.
Root Cause: The vibration vanished when the fan was running idle between heats. We balanced the rotor, but the problem persisted. The root cause was thermal bowing of the shaft. During the peak of the oxygen blow, the off-gas temperatures entering the fan spiked from 60°C to 180°C. One of the water-washing spray nozzles inside the fan casing had clogged. This caused the hot, dirty gas to heat one side of the massive steel impeller more than the other. The differential expansion physically bowed the rotor shaft by a few thousandths of an inch, throwing the 10-ton assembly completely out of balance at 1500 RPM.
Fix: Entered the fan casing, cleared the clogged washing spray nozzles, and replaced the spray header water filter.
Pro-Tip: Vibration that only happens at high temperatures is almost always uneven thermal expansion, not a lost balance weight. Check your cooling sprays.
