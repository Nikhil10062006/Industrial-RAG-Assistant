Problem: Blast Furnace top gas pressure spiked violently from 1.5 bar to 2.8 bar in under 3 seconds, triggering the emergency mechanical bleeder valves to blow open with a deafening roar.
Root Cause: The shift supervisor initially suspected a blockage in the gas cleaning plant (scrubber), which would back up pressure. However, the scrubber differential pressure was normal. The actual cause was a massive "slip" inside the furnace. The raw material burden had hung up (bridged) near the belly of the furnace, creating a void underneath. When the bridge finally collapsed under its own weight, hundreds of tons of material fell at once, violently displacing the hot gas below it and firing it up the shaft like a piston.
Fix: Reduced the hot blast wind volume by 30% to stabilize the furnace and prevent another slip. Allowed the bleeder valves to reset automatically. We then reviewed the burden distribution matrix and increased the coke ratio on the central rings to improve bed permeability.
Pro-Tip: A sudden pressure spike paired with a sudden drop in the stockline radar reading is a slip. Never force maximum wind volume if the stockline isn't descending smoothly.
