Electrode-column nip pressure dropped during a lift. Hydraulic pressure at the skid was normal, but a local accumulator isolation valve was partly closed. The column was stabilized and movement stopped before inspection. The valve was restored, accumulator pressure checked, and the nip tested without load. Record electrode size, pressure, lift condition, accumulator status, and test result. Do not continue lifting with marginal grip; a slipping electrode can damage the furnace roof or break into the bath.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
