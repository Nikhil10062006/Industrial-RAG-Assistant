Symptom: heat numbers painted on billets faded after rain and could not be read at the mill entry. The paint was correct but applied to a wet, scaled surface and the spray nozzle was partially blocked. The affected material was held and identity rebuilt from yard maps, crane records, caster sequence, and remaining marks. The paint system was corrected, surfaces were prepared, and a weather-resistant marker trial completed. Record material, marking point, paint batch, weather, readability, and disposition. Veteran note: a mark that survives a dry yard can fail after hot handling and rain. Do not repaint over an uncertain identity. Traceability must be recovered before release, and the new mark must be independently checked. The problem was added to the monsoon yard inspection.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
