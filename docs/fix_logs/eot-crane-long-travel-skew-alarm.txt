Symptom: The crane skewed slightly toward the north rail during long travel and tripped on anti-skew. Short travel was normal.

Finding: One rail wheel flange had accumulated scale and the wheel alignment was marginal. The encoder difference increased only over long travel. The anti-skew system was working correctly.

Fix: Parked and isolated the crane, cleaned the rail and wheel, measured wheel alignment, and corrected the drive-coupling setting. Long-travel testing was done unloaded before the crane was released for ladle service.

Lesson: Do not defeat anti-skew because the crane moves straight for ten metres. Long travel reveals rail, wheel, and encoder differences. A crane carrying a suspended ladle must be stopped and investigated at the first repeat alarm.

Rail gauge, wheel flange condition, brake release, and encoder counts were checked during the same inspection. The crane was released for light duty first and then tested without a load on the full route. Any repeat skew alarm now requires a planned inspection before ladle service.
