Symptom: the ladle preheater flame failed twice during ignition, but gas pressure at the skid was normal. The flame scanner showed no flame, and the burner management system locked out. Inspection found the pilot air regulator was set low after a maintenance test, producing an unstable pilot. The ignition transformer and gas valve were healthy. The burner was purged according to the approved sequence, the regulator was restored, and the flame was proven through the normal permissive chain. No one attempted repeated ignition without purge. Tribal knowledge: a flame-failure alarm can be caused by pilot air, scanner cleanliness, ignition gap, gas quality, or actual loss of fuel. Check the sequence and companion signals before replacing the scanner. Record purge time, gas pressure, pilot air pressure, scanner status, and number of attempts. A preheater that lights once is not accepted until it remains stable through the full warm-up period.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
