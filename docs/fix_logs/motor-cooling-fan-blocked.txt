A motor ran hot at normal load. Current balance was acceptable, but the cooling fan intake was blocked with dust and the shroud had shifted. The motor was stopped, cleaned, and insulation checked before restart. Record load, temperature, current, airflow, dust, and fan condition. A motor can pass electrical tests and still overheat from poor ventilation. Do not reduce overload setting to hide the temperature; correct cooling and confirm the load.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
