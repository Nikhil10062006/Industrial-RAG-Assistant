Symptom: drum level oscillated during a load change even though feedwater pressure was stable. The level transmitter was healthy, but steam-flow compensation had the wrong scaling after a control-system update. The feedwater valve responded too aggressively to apparent shrink and swell. The boiler was held at a safe load while the signals were compared with independent gauges and mass-flow references. Scaling was corrected, the loop was returned to the approved tuning, and a controlled load change was performed. Record drum level, steam flow, feedwater flow, valve position, transmitter values, and program revision. Veteran note: boiler level can move opposite to the actual water inventory during rapid pressure changes. Do not chase the visible level without understanding shrink and swell. Any control change must be reviewed and tested under a defined load range.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
