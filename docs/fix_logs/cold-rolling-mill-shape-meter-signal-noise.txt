Symptom: shape-meter output became noisy after an electrical panel modification. The strip itself looked stable. Inspection found the signal cable shield was bonded incorrectly at both ends and routed alongside a high-current drive cable. The sensor head was clean and aligned. The cable route and grounding were corrected, and the signal was checked with the mill unloaded and running. The shape control was kept in the approved fallback mode until quality accepted the data. Veteran note: a noisy shape signal is not a reason to chase roll bending. Compare sensor output with flatness appearance and inspect grounding, shielding, and cable separation. Record signal amplitude, drive state, cable route, grounding points, and before-after trend. Automation changes require signal-integrity checks, not only PLC logic checks.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
