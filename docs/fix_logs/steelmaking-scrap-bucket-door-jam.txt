Symptom: the scrap bucket door would not open fully above the furnace. Hydraulic pressure rose and the cylinder stopped. The bucket had a long piece of rebar trapped across the door edge. The crane load was stable, but the operator stopped instead of shaking the bucket. The bucket was moved to the approved safe position, the crane was isolated from unnecessary movement, and the obstruction was removed using the designated tool and exclusion zone. The cylinder, hinges, pins, and door stops were inspected before return. Veteran note: do not use crane travel or sudden bucket movement to free a jammed door. A trapped piece can release like a spring or damage the hinge. Record bucket number, material type, door position, pressure, obstruction, and inspection result. Scrap preparation and hollow-section rejection remain the preventive controls; the hydraulic circuit is not the place to solve a material problem.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
