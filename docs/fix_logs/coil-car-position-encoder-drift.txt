Symptom: the coil car stopped short of the loading saddle even though the screen showed target position. The encoder coupling had loosened and slipped intermittently. The car wheels, rails, and drive were mechanically sound. The car was isolated, the coupling repaired, and position verified against fixed marks at several travel points. The automatic mode was not re-enabled until the stopping distance and limit switches were checked. Record car number, commanded position, actual position, encoder status, wheel condition, and test route. Tribal knowledge: if the error changes after direction reversal, inspect backlash and coupling before replacing the encoder. Do not adjust the hard stop to make a bad position signal look correct. A mispositioned coil can damage saddles, straps, cranes, and people working in the bay.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
