Problem: The Tandem Cold Mill was producing coils with severe brown, patchy stains that became visible only after the coils came out of the batch annealing furnaces.
Root Cause: The metallurgical lab blamed the annealing furnace gas atmosphere, but the furnace logs were perfect. We investigated the rolling emulsion (the oil/water mix used to cool and lubricate the cold mill rolls). The emulsion tank concentration was correct at 3%, but a dip-slide test revealed massive anaerobic bacterial growth in the underground reservoir. The bacteria were eating the emulsifier additives and dropping the pH from a healthy 6.5 down to 4.5. This acidic, degraded emulsion was micro-rusting the steel strip while it sat in the storage yard waiting for annealing.
Fix: We had to dump the entire 50,000-liter emulsion tank, steam-clean the concrete reservoir to kill the biofilm, and recharge with fresh deionized water and rolling oil. Added a shock dose of biocide.
Pro-Tip: Emulsion isn't just oil and water; it's a living soup. If the pH drops unexpectedly, you have a bacterial infection. Smell the tank—if it smells like rotten eggs, dump it before you ruin 10,000 tons of steel.
