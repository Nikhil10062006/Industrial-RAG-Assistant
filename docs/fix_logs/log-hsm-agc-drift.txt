Problem: The Hot Strip Mill Automatic Gauge Control (AGC) on Stand F5 was slowly drifting. The hydraulic cylinders were creeping downward by 0.1mm every minute without any command from the pulpit, crushing the steel strip out of tolerance.
Root Cause: The automation engineers suspected an encoder drift or a PLC analog output card failure. We forced the command signal to absolute zero, but the cylinders kept creeping. The root cause was fluid bypassing inside the Moog servo valve. The main spool inside the valve had worn microscopically due to particulate contamination in the hydraulic oil. Even at a zero-command state, high-pressure fluid was leaking past the spool lands directly into the cylinder's extend port, acting like a slow hydraulic jack.
Fix: Swapped out the $15,000 Moog servo valve for a newly rebuilt unit and ran a kidney-loop offline filtration cart on the hydraulic tank for 48 hours to drop the ISO particle count.
Pro-Tip: If a hydraulic cylinder moves when the command is zero, your servo valve spool is passing fluid. Replace it before it fails wide open.
