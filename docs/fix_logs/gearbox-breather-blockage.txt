Symptom: gearbox oil leaked from a shaft seal and temperature rose slightly. Oil level was high after a recent top-up. Inspection found the breather blocked with dust and paint, allowing pressure to build as the gearbox warmed. The gearbox was isolated, oil level corrected, breather replaced, and shaft seal inspected. Record oil grade, level, temperature, pressure or vent condition, seal state, and load. Veteran note: a leaking seal is not always a bad seal. Check the breather before ordering parts. Overfilling and blocked vents are common after maintenance. Do not remove a breather from a running gearbox unless the task is specifically safe and authorized. Trend temperature and leakage after restart; pressure may take time to normalize.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
