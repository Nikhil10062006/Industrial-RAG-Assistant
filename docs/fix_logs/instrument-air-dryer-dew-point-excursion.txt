Symptom: instrument-air dew point rose above the operating band during automatic dryer changeover. Pressure remained normal, but several positioners began responding slowly. The standby dryer had a failed regeneration valve and the purge flow was low. The system was stabilized by returning the healthy dryer to service and reducing non-essential air demand. The failed valve was replaced, filters drained, and dew point checked at the far-end header as well as the dryer outlet. Record dryer state, purge pressure, outlet dew point, far-end dew point, valve feedback, and affected equipment. Veteran note: the compressor-room analyzer can look normal while moisture reaches the plant through a bypass or distribution low point. Sample at the user when the symptom is downstream. Do not drain instrument lines casually; follow the air-quality procedure and keep control valves stable.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
