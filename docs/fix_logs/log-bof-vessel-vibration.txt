Problem: Severe shuddering and vibration in the Basic Oxygen Furnace (BOF) primary tilt drives when returning the vessel from the horizontal tapping position back to the vertical blowing position.
Root Cause: The electrical team wasted a shift checking the VFDs and motor encoders. The problem was purely mechanical, but hidden. The space between the BOF steel shell and the massive trunnion ring that holds it is designed to allow for thermal expansion. Over months, liquid slag had splashed over the lip, run down the vessel side, and frozen solid inside this expansion gap. The vessel expanded as it heated up, but the frozen slag bound it rigidly to the trunnion ring. As the vessel tilted, this bind created massive torsional stress that abruptly slipped, sending shockwaves through the gearbox.
Fix: Tilted the vessel to 45 degrees. Had the refractory team use heavy pneumatic chipping hammers to break out the slag from the gap.
Pro-Tip: Check the trunnion air gap weekly. If it fills with slag, the vessel will eventually tear its own drive pins clean off.
