Problem: Blast furnace slag granulation plant was exhausting a highly visible, thick yellowish plume instead of the normal white steam plume.
Root Cause: The granulation process works by blasting high-pressure water into liquid slag at 1500°C to shatter it into glass-like sand. This generates massive amounts of steam. Usually, this steam is pure white. The yellow tint indicated high levels of elemental sulfur and H2S gas being released. The root cause was poor water chemistry. The cooling tower pH had dropped to 5.5 due to a failed caustic dosing pump. The acidic water reacted violently with the sulfur-rich blast furnace slag, stripping the sulfur out as a toxic gas instead of keeping it bound in the solid slag sand.
Fix: Restored the caustic dosing to bring the granulation water loop pH back to 7.5. The plume returned to pure white within 20 minutes.
Pro-Tip: The color of the steam plume is your best real-time chemistry indicator. Yellow means acid. Fix the water pH immediately.
