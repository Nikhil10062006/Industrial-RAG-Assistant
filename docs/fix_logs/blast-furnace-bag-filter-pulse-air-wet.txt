Bag-filter differential pressure rose after compressed air became wet. Pulse valves moved, but wet dust blinded the bags. The air dryer and drains were corrected, and the filter was operated at controlled load while pressure recovered. Record dew point, filter pressure, pulse pressure, hopper temperature, and dust condition. Wet pulse air can create a gradual restriction. Do not increase pulse pressure beyond the approved range.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
