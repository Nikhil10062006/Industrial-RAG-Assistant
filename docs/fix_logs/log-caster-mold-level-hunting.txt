Problem: Continuous Caster Strand 3 mold level was hunting aggressively by +/- 15mm. The stopper rod was modulating wildly trying to keep up.
Root Cause: The mold level is measured by a Cobalt-60 radioactive isotope on one side of the mold and a scintillation counter (receiver) on the other. Steel blocking the radiation path tells us the level. The instrument techs suspected a faulty stopper rod servo. I pulled the calibration logs for the Co-60 source. The source was 5.5 years old. Cobalt-60 has a half-life of 5.27 years. The radiation emission had simply dropped too low for the receiver to get a clear signal through the steel mold plates, causing signal noise that the PLC interpreted as level fluctuations.
Fix: We couldn't replace the source mid-cast. The instrument tech increased the signal integration time constant in the PLC from 0.1s to 0.5s to smooth out the noise, which stabilized the level enough to finish the sequence. Scheduled a source replacement for the down-day.
Pro-Tip: When radiometric sensors get "noisy," always check the source age before tearing apart the hydraulics.
