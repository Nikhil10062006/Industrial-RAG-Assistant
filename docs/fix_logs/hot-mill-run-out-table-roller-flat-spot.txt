A periodic mark appeared on the underside of slabs after a table stoppage. One roller had a flat spot from sitting under a hot slab without rotation. The roller was replaced and the table checked for free rotation. Record roller number, pitch, slab temperature, stop duration, and defect window. A mark that follows roller circumference can persist until the roller is changed. Do not release material made during the first restart without inspection.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
