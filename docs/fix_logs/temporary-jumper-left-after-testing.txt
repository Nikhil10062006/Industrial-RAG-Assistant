Operating area: Mechanical, electrical, automation, and workshop maintenance. Measure the condition with correct tools and record units, speed, load, temperature, pressure, current, vibration, or signal state. Do not rely on touch or sound alone where exposure exists. A temporary PLC force remained active after a commissioning test. Remove the force, document the test, and perform a cold-start interlock check. The commissioning checklist now requires clearing and proving all forces. Record the equipment tag, material or heat identity, time, readings with units, people informed, and the final status. If a temporary control, bypass, manual setting, or alternate route is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not close the event because the alarm cleared or the equipment restarted once. Repeat the relevant test under the same load, speed, temperature, pressure, or material condition that caused the problem. At handover, state what was checked, what remains uncertain, and the exact condition that requires stopping the equipment. Practical plant knowledge is useful when it makes the next safe decision easier without replacing isolation, permit, quality, emergency, or engineering controls.
