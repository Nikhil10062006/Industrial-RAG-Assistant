Problem: Wire Rod Mill laying head (coiler) experienced a massive tail-end cobble. The front of the wire rod coiled perfectly, but the last 50 meters lost tension and bunched up into a bird's nest inside the laying head pipe.
Root Cause: The pinch rolls immediately upstream of the laying head are supposed to clamp down hard on the tail end to maintain tension as the rod exits the final mill stand. We checked the pinch roll pneumatic cylinders; they were actuating, but weakly. Discovered that the local nitrogen accumulator bottle, which provides the sudden burst of pressure required to snap the pinch rolls closed, had lost its pre-charge. It was sitting at 5 bar instead of the required 45 bar.
Fix: Recharged the nitrogen accumulator bottle. Replaced a leaking Schrader valve on top of the bladder that caused the slow pressure loss.
Pro-Tip: If you only cobble on the tail ends, your pinch roll response time is too slow. It's almost always a flat accumulator. Check your pre-charges weekly.
