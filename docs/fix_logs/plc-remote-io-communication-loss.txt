Operating area: Mechanical, electrical, automation, and workshop maintenance. Start with safe isolation, equipment identity, stored energy, and a preserved fault history. Obtain field evidence before changing parts or logic. Remote I/O dropped when a network switch power supply sagged. Compare rack power, network link, I/O status, and cabinet temperature. The network event was escalated as a power-quality issue, not just a PLC fault. Record the equipment tag, material or heat identity, time, readings with units, people informed, and the final status. If a temporary control, bypass, manual setting, or alternate route is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not close the event because the alarm cleared or the equipment restarted once. Repeat the relevant test under the same load, speed, temperature, pressure, or material condition that caused the problem. At handover, state what was checked, what remains uncertain, and the exact condition that requires stopping the equipment. Practical plant knowledge is useful when it makes the next safe decision easier without replacing isolation, permit, quality, emergency, or engineering controls.
