Symptom: recirculating cooling-water conductivity rose above the operating band while flow and pressure remained normal. Strainers were clean, but nozzle plugging had increased over several heats. The makeup-water valve had failed partly closed and the blowdown sequence had not completed. Laboratory results confirmed concentration of dissolved solids. The system was stabilized by reducing heat load as required, restoring makeup and blowdown, and checking water chemistry before full production. Nozzle and filter condition were inspected after the water returned to range. Record conductivity, pH, temperature, makeup flow, blowdown status, and equipment symptoms. Tribal knowledge: conductivity is a trend alarm before it becomes a visible cooling failure. Do not solve it by opening a random drain or adding untreated water without the water-treatment owner. High concentration can damage valves, clog sprays, and reduce heat transfer even when the main flow transmitter looks healthy.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
