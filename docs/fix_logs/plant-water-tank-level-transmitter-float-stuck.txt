Tank level remained high on the screen while local sight glass was low. The transmitter float was fouled by biological growth and the high-level alarm had not activated. The tank was manually controlled under the water procedure, float cleaned, and alarm tested. Record level comparison, float condition, inlet flow, outlet flow, and alarm test. A stuck level can cause pump dry-run or overflow. Compare screen, local, and independent measurement before changing control logic.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
