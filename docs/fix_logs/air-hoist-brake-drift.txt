An air hoist lowered slightly after the control was released. Supply pressure was normal, but the brake lining was oily and the exhaust muffler restricted release. The hoist was removed from service, brake cleaned or replaced, and load-tested. Record hoist, load, pressure, drift, brake condition, and test. A small drift with an empty hook becomes serious with a motor or pump suspended. Never continue because the hoist stops after a short movement.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
