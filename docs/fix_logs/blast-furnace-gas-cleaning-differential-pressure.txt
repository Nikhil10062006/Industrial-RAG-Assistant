Symptom: gas-cleaning differential pressure rose over one shift while top pressure control became sluggish. The dust-catcher level indication was normal, so the first assumption was a transmitter drift. Comparison with local pressure points showed the transmitter was correct. Inspection found a partially closed dust-discharge valve after maintenance, restricting solids removal and loading the gas path. The valve position feedback had been left in manual and did not match the field position. The line was stabilized, the valve was returned to the approved position, and the dust level was managed through the safe discharge procedure. Differential pressure fell gradually rather than immediately because accumulated dust had to clear. The control system was corrected to alarm on disagreement between field and remote position. Veteran note: a gas-cleaning restriction may show first as poor top-pressure response, not a dramatic high differential-pressure alarm. Check dust discharge, spray water, wet scrubber level, and impulse lines before changing a pressure loop. Gas areas require detector and access controls even when the process looks stable.
