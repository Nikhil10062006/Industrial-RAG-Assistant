Foamy slag collapsed suddenly and arc flare increased. Oxygen and carbon injection settings had not changed, but scrap chemistry and slag basicity were different from the previous heat. The furnace was operated under the approved reduced-exposure condition while samples and trends were reviewed. Record arc current, oxygen, carbon, slag height, pressure, and heat chemistry. Veteran operators know a quiet arc can be a good sign, but a sudden noisy arc with falling foam needs attention. Do not correct by changing several injection rates at once.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
