Problem: Blast Furnace belly stave cooling circuit #12 showed a sudden drop in flow from 120 L/min down to 15 L/min. Supply pressure remained perfectly normal at 6 bar.
Root Cause: The junior operator assumed the main supply valve had vibrated closed, but it was physically locked open. The stave had suffered a "vapor lock." There was a localized loss of protective buildup inside the furnace, exposing the copper stave to massive heat flux. The water inside the stave boiled instantly. Because steam expands 1600 times the volume of water, the steam bubble created massive back-pressure, completely blocking the incoming water flow despite the 6 bar supply pressure.
Fix: We hooked up a high-pressure nitrogen bottle to the purge port on the supply header. We shot a 15-bar nitrogen pulse directly into the stave. The pressure spike collapsed the steam bubble and forced it out the return line, instantly restoring the water flow.
Pro-Tip: When flow drops but supply pressure is stable on a hot furnace panel, it's vapor lock. Never ignore it; if you don't collapse the bubble within 30 minutes, the copper will melt.
