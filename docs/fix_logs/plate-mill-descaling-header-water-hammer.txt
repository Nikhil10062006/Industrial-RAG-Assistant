Symptom: a loud bang occurred when a plate-mill descaling header opened, followed by a pressure spike and a loose support clamp. The header had been drained for maintenance and refilled rapidly. Air was trapped at a high point, and the actuator opened faster than the old valve. The system was isolated and the support inspected before production resumed. Air was vented through the designated point, the actuator stroke time was adjusted, and the header was filled slowly. Record pressure peak, valve timing, trapped-air location, support damage, and test result. Do not dismiss one bang if the pipe appears intact; supports, flanges, nozzles, and pump seals may have been stressed. Future maintenance requires a slow-fill and venting step. Water hammer is a system event, not merely a noisy valve.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
