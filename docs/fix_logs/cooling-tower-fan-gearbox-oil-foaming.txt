Symptom: gearbox oil sight glass showed foam and the fan vibration trend increased. Oil level was not low. Inspection found the wrong viscosity oil had been added during a top-up and the breather was blocked with dust. The fan was reduced or stopped according to the temperature plan. Oil was drained to the approved level, the breather cleaned, and the gearbox inspected for water and bearing damage. Record oil type, level, temperature, vibration, breather condition, and fan load. A foamy sight glass can indicate overfill, wrong oil, water ingress, air entrainment, or mechanical churning. Do not add more oil because the level “looks low” through foam. Check the manufacturer specification and sample condition. After restart, trend vibration and temperature through a complete warm period.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
