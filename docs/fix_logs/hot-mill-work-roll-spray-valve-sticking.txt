One cooling bank remained open after the command closed, causing a strip temperature dip. The valve actuator feedback was intermittent and the valve stem had scale. The line was stabilized, the valve isolated, and travel checked locally. Record bank, pressure, command, feedback, actual position, and strip temperature. Do not alter the cooling recipe to compensate for a valve that is not following the command. A field position check is required before replacing the controller.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
