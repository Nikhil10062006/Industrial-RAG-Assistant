Symptom: segment-roll vibration rose after a cooling-water hose replacement. The bearing temperature was initially normal. Inspection found a misaligned hose spraying directly at a bearing seal. Water entered the housing and diluted the grease. The segment was removed during the planned window, the bearing inspected, and contaminated lubricant replaced. The hose was rerouted and clamped away from the seal. The roll was rotated by hand and checked for roughness before reinstallation. Veteran note: water-related bearing damage can start with a normal temperature and become obvious only after the lubricant is displaced. Look for milky grease, rust staining, seal lip damage, and spray direction. Record roll position, hose change, vibration, temperature, lubricant condition, and follow-up readings. A new hose is not automatically a correct hose installation; its bend radius and discharge direction matter.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
