Symptom: demineralized-water conductivity rose after a resin-bed change. The online analyzer alarmed, but tank level and flow were normal. A sampling valve had been left open to drain, allowing untreated water to mix with the product header. The affected header was isolated, users were informed, and the tank was held for laboratory confirmation. The valve lineup was corrected and the bed rinse sequence repeated. Record conductivity, sample temperature, valve positions, bed number, rinse time, and users exposed. Tribal knowledge: a sudden conductivity step after maintenance often points to lineup or sample problems before resin exhaustion. Verify analyzer calibration and take a representative sample, but treat the high reading as real until proven otherwise. Do not send questionable water to boiler or instrument service.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
