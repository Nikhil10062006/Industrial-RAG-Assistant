The pendant crane stopped responding in one travel direction. Radio control and local panel were healthy. Inspection found a damaged pendant cable strain relief and intermittent conductor at the festoon end. The crane was parked safely, isolated, and the cable replaced. Test all directions, horn, limit switches, and emergency stop after repair. Do not continue on radio while the pendant fault remains unexplained if both controls share a circuit. Record crane, command, cable location, alarm, and test result. Intermittent controls are more dangerous than a complete failure because operators may repeat commands.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
