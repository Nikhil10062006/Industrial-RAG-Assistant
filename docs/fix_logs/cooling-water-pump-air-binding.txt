Symptom: a standby cooling-water pump started but delivered low flow with fluctuating discharge pressure. The pump casing was noisy and motor current was low. The suction line had been drained during maintenance and not fully vented, leaving an air pocket. The pump was stopped, isolated, vented through the approved point, and restarted with the suction flooded. Record suction pressure, discharge pressure, flow, current, vent condition, and tank level. Veteran note: low current plus noise and low flow can mean the pump is moving air, not that the motor is underloaded normally. Do not continue running a dry or air-bound pump. Check suction valve, strainer, foot valve, tank level, and high-point vent after every line break.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
