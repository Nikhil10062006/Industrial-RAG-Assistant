Symptom: repeating dents appeared on the strip after a pinch-roll change. Roll diameter and gap were correct. Inspection found a small weld spatter particle embedded in the roll surface near the operator side. The roll was removed, dressed within the approved limit, and rechecked for runout and surface finish. The strip made during the affected interval was held. Tribal knowledge: a tiny raised defect on a pinch roll can produce a large repeating mark and may not be obvious under mill lighting. Check pitch against roll circumference. Do not polish a roll in place or use a hand tool near a moving pinch point. Record roll number, mark pitch, strip speed, roll surface, replacement action, and quality disposition. A clean roll change does not guarantee a clean surface; inspect after transport and installation.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
