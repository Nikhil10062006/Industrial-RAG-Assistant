Symptom: ultrasonic thickness readings jumped high and low on a hot plate. The probe face was clean, but couplant boiled away intermittently and the surface scale was uneven. The operator was pressing harder, which distorted the reading. The test was paused, the surface prepared, temperature-rated couplant used, and calibration checked on a reference block at similar temperature. Record probe, couplant, surface condition, temperature, calibration, grid, and rejected readings. Do not accept a single stable value if signal quality is poor. Veteran note: a plausible number with weak coupling is still unreliable. If the surface is too hot or rough for the method, escalate and use the approved alternative.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
