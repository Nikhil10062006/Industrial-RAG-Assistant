Transformer oil temperature rose during a normal heat while current was within plan. Cooler fan status was healthy, but one oil-cooler valve was partly closed after inspection. The load was reduced under the electrical procedure, valve lineup corrected, and oil temperature trended through a full heat. Record current, oil temperature, cooler flow, fan status, and valve position. A healthy transformer alarm does not mean cooling is adequate. Check heat removal before changing electrical settings.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
