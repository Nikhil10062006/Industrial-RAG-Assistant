Symptom: fuel-gas pressure downstream of a regulator fell during high demand and the regulator body showed frost. Upstream pressure was stable. Moisture in the gas stream expanded and cooled at the regulator, causing restriction. The system was moved to the approved reduced-load state and the regulator bypass was not opened blindly. The upstream separator drain and heater were checked, and the regulator was changed over under the gas procedure. Record upstream and downstream pressure, gas temperature, demand, frost location, separator condition, and regulator position. Tribal knowledge: frost is a clue to pressure drop and moisture, not an invitation to pour hot water on the valve. Check gas quality, filtration, heating, and pressure drop. Any changeover must preserve flame supervision and avoid an ignition source near a leaking connection.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
