Symptom: a conveyor stopped on pull-cord fault with no visible emergency condition. The switch lever was in the normal position, but the cord tension was low after a transfer-chute repair. The switch had not reset fully and vibration opened the contact intermittently. The line was isolated, cord alignment and tension were corrected, and every switch was tested from both sides. Record conveyor, switch number, cord length, tension, reset behavior, and test result. Tribal knowledge: a pull-cord alarm can look like an electrical fault while the real issue is cord geometry. Do not bypass the safety circuit to keep material moving. Check the entire cord route, turnbuckles, guides, switch reset, and cable glands. A safety switch that trips too easily is still a safety defect until the root cause is corrected.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
