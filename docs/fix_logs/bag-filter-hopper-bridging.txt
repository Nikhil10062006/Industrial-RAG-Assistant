Symptom: a bag-filter hopper level stayed high while the rotary valve motor current remained low. Dust discharge was poor and differential pressure rose. Inspection found moist dust bridged above the outlet; the rotary valve was turning without receiving material. The fan and filter were placed in the approved safe condition, the hopper isolated, and the bridge cleared using the designed method. No person entered or struck the hopper. The rotary valve seal and heater were checked. Record hopper level, dust moisture, outlet temperature, rotary-valve current, differential pressure, and action. Veteran note: a running discharge motor does not prove the hopper is emptying. Moisture, low temperature, and failed heat tracing often combine. Do not open an inspection hatch while dust is suspended or the system is pressurized.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
