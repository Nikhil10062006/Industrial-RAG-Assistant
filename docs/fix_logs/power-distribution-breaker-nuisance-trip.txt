Symptom: A low-voltage feeder breaker tripped during simultaneous starts of two large pumps. The breaker had carried each pump individually without issue.

Finding: The instantaneous protection setting was too close to the combined inrush current after a motor replacement. The motor was correctly wired but had a higher starting current than the previous unit.

Fix: Reviewed the coordination study, verified cable and motor ratings, and adjusted the protection only within the approved engineering setting. A soft-start sequence was added so the pumps did not start together. The breaker and termination temperatures were checked under load.

Lesson: Do not increase a breaker setting because production wants a reset. Confirm motor data, starting method, cable capacity, and protection coordination. A nuisance trip may be warning that the system has lost its intended selectivity.

The revised start sequence was tested with the actual replacement motor and normal downstream load. Protection settings were sealed and recorded after approval. Any future motor substitution must trigger a coordination review before energization, even if the nameplate power is the same.
