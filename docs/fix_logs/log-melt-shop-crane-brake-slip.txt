Problem: The 250-ton Hot Metal Charging Crane experienced a load slip. While holding a full ladle of iron waiting for the BOF to tilt, the hoist physically dropped 1 meter before the secondary caliper brakes violently arrested it.
Root Cause: Electrical maintenance checked the primary shoe brakes and found the voltage holding them open was dropping out correctly. The issue was the brake lining friction pads. The night shift operator had a habit of "riding the brake"—feathering the joystick to inch the load down against the mechanical brakes instead of using the VFD dynamic braking. This friction superheated the brake pads, causing the resin in the linings to melt and glaze over into a glassy, frictionless surface.
Fix: Mechanical fitters had to lock out the hoist, remove the massive brake shoes, and physically use heavy-grit belt sanders to strip the glazing off the pads down to fresh friction material.
Pro-Tip: Never use mechanical brakes to control speed; that is what the VFD is for. The mechanical brake is a holding parking brake ONLY.
