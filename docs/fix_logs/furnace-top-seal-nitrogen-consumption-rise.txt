A rising seal-gas consumption with stable furnace pressure was traced to a leaking rotary joint seal. The remote flow indication was correct, but the local sound was hidden by charging noise. The top equipment was stabilized, the joint isolated, and the seal replaced. Consumption returned to baseline after the chute moved through its full range. Record flow, pressure, chute angle, joint condition, and detector readings. If consumption rises only at one angle, inspect flexible hoses, rotary joints, and carriers before changing the regulator. Do not enter the top area without gas controls.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
