Symptom: a sharp clicking sound and diameter variation appeared after a sizing-roll change. Inspection found a hairline crack at the roll shoulder, likely initiated by a previous impact. The roll had passed visual inspection under poor lighting. The line was stopped and the roll removed under the lifting plan. Dye-penetrant inspection confirmed the crack. The roll was scrapped and the stand alignment checked before a new set was installed. Record roll number, material, diameter, crack location, handling history, and inspection method. A cracked roll can fragment at speed, so do not run at reduced speed “just to finish the coil.” Veteran note: changes in sound plus periodic dimensional variation deserve a stop. Check roll seating, keyway, impact marks, and transport supports after every change.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
