Symptom: water appeared around the descaler pump seal and header pressure began to pulse. The pump was still delivering flow, so production continued briefly. Inspection found abrasive scale in the seal flush line and a worn mechanical seal. The pump was isolated, drained, and replaced with the standby after checking suction conditions. The flush filter was cleaned and the source of scale ingress reviewed. The pressure trend stabilized at the normal range. Veteran note: a seal leak plus pressure ripple is an equipment stop signal, not a housekeeping issue. High-pressure water can cut skin and carry scale at speed. Record pump current, discharge pressure, seal leakage, flush pressure, filter condition, and time to changeover. If the same seal fails repeatedly, review water quality, alignment, shaft sleeve, and flush arrangement instead of changing seals alone.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
