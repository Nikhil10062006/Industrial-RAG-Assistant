Problem: The Blast Furnace Gas (BFG) holder level indicator froze at 45%. Gas was actively being pumped into the holder, but the telemetry didn't move. The risk of overfilling and blowing the membrane seal was critical.
Root Cause: The BFG holder uses a massive, floating piston roof. The height is measured by a simple mechanical wire-rope encoder. A steel cable runs from the floating roof, over a pulley at the top of the holder, down to an encoder wheel on the ground. A heavy pigeon had flown into the pulley housing at the top of the 100-meter structure and died, wedging its body between the wire rope and the sheave wheel. The cable was physically jammed, holding the encoder perfectly still while the massive roof continued to rise underneath it, putting the cable under extreme tension.
Fix: Switched the gas network to manual pressure control. A technician climbed the 100-meter exterior stairway, cleared the bird carcass, and reset the cable onto the sheave.
Pro-Tip: Low-tech sensors fail for low-tech reasons. If a wire-rope encoder freezes, look for physical jams on the pulleys before calling the software team.
