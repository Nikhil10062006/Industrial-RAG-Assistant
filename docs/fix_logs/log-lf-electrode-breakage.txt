Problem: A 400mm graphite electrode snapped clean off in the Ladle Furnace (LF) during the secondary heating cycle. The broken piece fell into the liquid steel bath, causing severe carbon pickup.
Root Cause: The automated electrode regulator panel showed a "high current / short circuit" fault just before the break. The mechanical team thought the mast carriage jammed. The real issue was the slag chemistry. The melt shop had sent over a ladle with a highly oxidized, crusty, and non-conductive slag layer. When the LF automation lowered the electrodes to strike the arc, the electrodes physically pushed into the solid slag crust without establishing an electrical arc. The hydraulic mast kept pushing down until the lateral bending force snapped the graphite.
Fix: Removed the broken graphite chunk using the auxiliary crane grab (wasted 45 minutes). Manually added fluorspar and aluminum to fluidize the slag before attempting to strike the arc again.
Pro-Tip: The automated regulator assumes it is lowering into liquid. If the slag looks black and crusty, switch to manual mode. Tap the electrodes on the crust slowly to strike the arc, let it melt a pool, and then switch to auto.
