Turbine lube-oil pressure fell briefly during a load change. The auxiliary pump started, but the main filter differential pressure was high and oil temperature low. The unit was stabilized and the filter changed under the turbine procedure. Record oil pressure, temperature, filter differential, pump status, and load. Do not reset a low-pressure trip repeatedly; bearing damage can occur before temperature rises. Verify pressure at the bearing header, not only at the pump discharge.  The equipment was not accepted only because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the trend was saved. The record states what was checked, what was changed, what remains unverified, and what would require another stop. This prevents a short reset from becoming an undocumented workaround. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
