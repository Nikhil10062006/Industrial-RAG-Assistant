Symptom: a bearing temperature alarm appeared while handheld infrared measurement remained normal. The sensor was loose in its pocket and not making consistent contact. The signal jumped when the cable moved. The machine was checked for actual vibration, sound, lubrication, and temperature before deciding it was an instrument fault. The sensor was reseated with the correct compound and strain relief. Record alarm value, handheld comparison, sensor seating, cable condition, bearing temperature, and vibration. Veteran note: a false high can still distract from a real bearing problem. Never dismiss a temperature alarm without checking the bearing by independent means. If the instrument is corrected, keep the machine under observation through a complete load cycle.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
