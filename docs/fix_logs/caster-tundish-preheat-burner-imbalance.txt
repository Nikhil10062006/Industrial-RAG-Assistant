Symptom: one tundish corner remained cooler while the burner package showed normal total gas flow. The flame from one burner was lifting and unstable. Inspection found a partially blocked burner tip and a loose air register. The burner was isolated and cleaned under the hot-work and gas controls. Air and gas settings were restored, and the tundish was held for a longer soak under the refractory instruction. The temperature map was checked with the approved reference. Tribal knowledge: a total gas-flow number does not show whether each burner is delivering a stable flame. A bright, noisy, lifting flame can be worse than a smaller stable flame. Record burner number, gas pressure, air pressure, flame appearance, tundish temperature map, and repair. Do not move a partially dried tundish into service because the schedule is tight; retained moisture can create a serious event when steel arrives.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
