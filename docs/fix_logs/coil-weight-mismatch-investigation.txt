Symptom: shipping weight differed from the production record by more than the normal tolerance. The scale display was stable, but the coil identification had been entered before the coil was fully supported on the weighbridge. The scale was checked with a certified test weight and passed. The coil was reweighed after the platform was clear and settled; the second result matched the material record. The system sequence was changed to require stable-weight status before identity confirmation. Record coil number, scale ID, tare, gross, net, stabilization time, and any strap or pallet weight. Veteran note: a good scale can still receive a bad measurement if the load is moving, touching a structure, or paired with the wrong tare. Do not change the scale calibration to match an incorrect transaction. Hold the shipment until traceability is resolved.

The equipment was not accepted merely because the alarm cleared. A controlled trial was run under the same load or process condition that produced the symptom, and the relevant trend was saved. The work record includes the tag, time, readings with units, recent maintenance, parts changed, and what remains unverified. If the condition returns, compare it with this baseline before changing another parameter. This detail matters because a short reset can hide a mechanical, process, or instrument problem.
