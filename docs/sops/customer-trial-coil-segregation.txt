Trial coils must be physically segregated, clearly tagged, and digitally marked with trial condition, approval status, and destination. Confirm the customer or process requirement before movement. Record heat, coil, trial parameter, inspection result, packaging, and release authority. A trial coil mixed with normal product can create a customer complaint even when its dimensions are correct. Do not remove the trial tag until quality closes the trial.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
