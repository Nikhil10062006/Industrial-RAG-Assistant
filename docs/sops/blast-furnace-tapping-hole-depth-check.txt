Before a planned cast, verify tap-hole depth using the approved drill and measurement method. Confirm drill alignment, hydraulic pressure, cooling-water flow, and exclusion-zone barriers. The cast-house controller must know whether the hole is short, normal, or deep before the mud gun is positioned. A short hole can open quickly and produce an unstable stream; a long or partially blocked hole can delay casting and increase hearth pressure. Never stand in line with the drill or tap-hole opening. If the drill meets unusual resistance, stop and inspect the bit, guide, and refractory condition from the safe position. Do not compensate by increasing thrust without authorization. Record hole depth, drilling time, bit condition, opening behavior, and the last cast duration. Veteran operators compare today’s depth with the previous two casts rather than relying on one number. A change after a repair, burden change, or prolonged low blast should be reported to the furnace controller. The measurement is useful only when the method and reference point are consistent.
