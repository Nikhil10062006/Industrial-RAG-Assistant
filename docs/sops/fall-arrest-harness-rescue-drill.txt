Conduct a harness-rescue drill using the approved training arrangement, not a live production edge. Review anchor selection, lanyard length, rescue kit, communication, access, and emergency number. Participants must understand that a suspended person may deteriorate quickly and that climbing to the person without a plan creates a second casualty. Check equipment tags, inspection, and compatibility before the drill. Time the response from alarm to simulated recovery and record obstacles such as locked gates, poor lighting, or missing keys. Do not use a crane hook or forklift as a rescue method unless the site plan specifically allows it. After the drill, restore the rescue kit and report defects. A harness is not a complete fall-protection system unless rescue is practical. Include contractors and night-shift personnel in the training.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
