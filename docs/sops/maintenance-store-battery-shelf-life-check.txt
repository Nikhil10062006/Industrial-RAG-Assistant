Before issuing a battery, verify chemistry, voltage, capacity, manufacture date, shelf life, storage temperature, terminal condition, and charge status. Do not install a battery with swelling, leakage, corrosion, or unknown history. For standby batteries, inspect charger voltage, electrolyte or indicator where applicable, ventilation, and terminal torque. Keep batteries away from sparks and incompatible chemicals. Record serial number, installation date, test result, and equipment tag. A battery can show acceptable open-circuit voltage and still fail under load. Perform the approved load or impedance test. Do not mix old and new cells in a critical bank without technical approval. Store batteries secured and protected from short circuits. The replacement record should state whether the failure was battery age, charger issue, temperature, loose connection, or repeated deep discharge.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
