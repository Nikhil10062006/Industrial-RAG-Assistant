Before work begins, verify that each lock and tag identifies the person, date, equipment, and contact required by the site rule. Tags must be readable, durable, and attached at the isolation point where they can be seen without entering danger. Do not accept an unmarked lock, a damaged tag, or a tag hanging from the wrong valve. Compare the field isolation with the permit and drawing. If a tag is missing or unclear, stop and resolve it with the isolation authority. Record isolation point, lock owner, test result, and any group-lockbox arrangement. A lock is a physical control; a tag explains it. Both matter during shift change, especially when several similar valves or feeders are adjacent.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
