After removing a roll, inspect the roll neck, sleeve, bearing fit, keyway, fillet, and seal land under good lighting. Clean oil and scale before judging the surface. Use the approved gauge for diameter and runout, and compare with the roll card. Scoring on the bearing fit can create eccentricity and heat even when the roll body looks perfect. Do not dress a critical surface without engineering limits. Check for raised metal around a keyway and for fretting marks at the chock interface. Record roll number, location, depth or extent of damage, diameter, runout, and disposition. A small burr can prevent proper seating and cause vibration at speed. If damage follows one chock, inspect both roll and chock rather than reusing the mating component automatically. Release requires a second-person review for critical rolls.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
