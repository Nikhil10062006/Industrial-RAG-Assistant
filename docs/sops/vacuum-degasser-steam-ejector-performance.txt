Before starting a steam ejector, check motive-steam pressure, condenser water, drains, valve lineup, vessel leak tightness, and interlocks. Open steam gradually and watch vacuum, temperature, condensate, and stage pressure. Water hammer or unstable vacuum means hold the sequence and clear drains. Record vacuum curve, steam pressure, condenser temperature, and heat number. A slow vacuum may be a leak, wet steam, poor condenser cooling, or a blocked nozzle. Do not increase steam blindly.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
