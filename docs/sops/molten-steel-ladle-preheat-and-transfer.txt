Before preheating or transferring a ladle, inspect the shell, bottom, trunnions, slag line, refractory condition, slide gate, shroud, and ladle identification. Check that the ladle is dry. Any water trapped in a pocket, tuyere, or repair area can flash to steam when steel arrives.

Verify the ladle preheat temperature using the approved method and record the result. Do not point a torch at a wet patch to “dry it faster.” If the ladle has been washed, repaired, or left exposed in rain, extend the drying and inspection period according to the refractory standard.

During transfer, keep personnel outside the marked hot-metal route. The crane operator and signalman must agree on one command system before lifting. Raise slowly, hold to check the balance, and avoid swinging. At the furnace or caster, confirm the receiving position is ready and the interlocks are healthy before opening the slide gate.

If a slide gate leaks, do not stand below or attempt an improvised adjustment. Stop the transfer, move to the safe position, and follow the ladle emergency plan. A small glowing stream is still a serious event. Record the ladle number, steel grade, preheat time, refractory observation, gate behavior, and any delay. The ladle history often explains a later breakout or premature lining failure.
