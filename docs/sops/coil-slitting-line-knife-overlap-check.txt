Before setting slitter knives, verify line isolation, arbor condition, knife diameter, separator arrangement, strip width, and job card. Use the approved gauge to set knife overlap and side clearance; keep hands away from sharp edges and loaded arbors. Check that knives are clean, undamaged, and correctly oriented. A wrong separator stack can produce burr, telescoping, or unstable narrow coils. Run the arbor slowly without strip and confirm no contact beyond the specified overlap. Thread at low speed and inspect the first slit edges. Record knife numbers, overlap, clearance, separator widths, strip grade, and first-coil results. Do not use a damaged knife because the setup is urgent. Veteran slitter operators compare burr direction and width distribution with the previous coil, but they still verify the physical stack. Locking nuts and spacers must be torqued to the approved value before production speed.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
