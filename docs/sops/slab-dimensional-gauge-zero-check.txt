Before measuring slabs, verify gauge zero, calibration status, temperature compensation, reference block, measuring force, and clean contact surfaces. Inspect the slab identity and measurement location. Scale, burr, and hot surfaces can produce a false dimension. Use the approved method and do not place hands near moving slabs or rolls. Compare the instrument with the reference at the start of shift and after impact, battery change, or temperature change. Record gauge ID, reference value, slab temperature, location, measured result, and operator. If the result differs from the process gauge, repeat with an independent method and hold the material when required. A stable wrong gauge is more dangerous than a noisy one because it can pass several pieces. Clean and store the instrument correctly after use.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
