For a spill, visible emission, fish kill, abnormal discharge, or permit alarm, protect people first, stop the source if safe, contain the material, and notify the area and environmental controllers. Record time, location, substance, estimated quantity, weather, route, and immediate action. Do not speculate publicly or alter evidence. Samples and photographs must be labeled and controlled. The first notification should be factual and prompt; investigation and reporting follow the approved process.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
