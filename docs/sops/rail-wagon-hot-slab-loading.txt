Before loading hot slabs onto rail wagons, confirm wagon rating, slab sequence, support spacing, load distribution, temperature condition, route clearance, and communication with rail control. The wagon must be inspected for damage, water, debris, and correct restraints. Do not load a wet wagon or place a hot slab against trapped water. Crane movement requires one designated signalman and a clear exclusion zone. Check the slab is fully seated and cannot roll or slide before releasing the hook. Record wagon, slab IDs, weights, temperature, support condition, and restraint. A hot slab can continue to radiate heat into the wagon and route, so inspect after loading. Do not improvise chains or blocks; use the approved restraint system.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
