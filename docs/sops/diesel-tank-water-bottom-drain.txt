Drain water from a diesel tank only from the approved low point with ignition control, spill containment, and the tank procedure. Sample the first discharge in the designated container and do not discharge to drains. Record water, sediment, fuel appearance, tank level, and date. A cloudy sample may indicate microbial growth or contamination. Close the valve before fuel appears and secure the drain. Do not stand over the outlet or use an open flame to inspect fuel.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
