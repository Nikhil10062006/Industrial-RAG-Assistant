Before a tension-control test, verify strip path, bridle condition, load cells, speed feedback, roll gap, and emergency stops. Use the approved test strip or no-strip mode. Compare commanded tension with load-cell indication at several setpoints and check for hysteresis when increasing and decreasing. Inspect the bridle roll surface and hydraulic pressure if the reading does not return to zero. Do not calibrate a load cell while the frame is touching a temporary tool or while the strip is under unknown tension. Record setpoint, actual tension, speed, direction, sensor channel, and test result. A stable bias can come from zero drift, roll slip, or incorrect strip width compensation. After calibration, run a short coil at low speed and confirm surface and shape. The tension loop should be understood before automatic control is enabled.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
