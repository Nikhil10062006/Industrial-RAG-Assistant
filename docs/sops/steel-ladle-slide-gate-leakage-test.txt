Before a ladle is released for steel, inspect the slide-gate frame, plates, hydraulic cylinder, refractory well block, collector nozzle, and locking devices. Confirm the ladle is dry and the gate assembly matches the grade and casting route. Perform the approved cold movement and seating test before preheat completion. The gate must open and close smoothly without abnormal pressure or sticking. Do not use a higher hydraulic pressure to overcome a mechanical bind. Check for plate cracks, chipped edges, loose fasteners, and refractory powder around the seating faces. During transfer, observe for seepage or glowing leakage from the collector nozzle. If leakage appears, move people to the safe position and follow the ladle emergency plan; do not stand below or attempt to tighten the assembly. Record ladle number, plate batch, test pressure, movement time, preheat condition, and final acceptance. A gate that moves correctly cold can still leak when heated if plates are damaged or the well block is not seated. The history must travel with the ladle, especially after repair.
