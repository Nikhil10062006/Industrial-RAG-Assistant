Each chemical area must have current safety-data information accessible without entering the hazard zone. Review container labels, transfer lines, emergency response, PPE, incompatibilities, and first aid. Remove obsolete copies and report missing language or unreadable labels. Record area, chemical, document revision, and action. An SDS is useful only when it matches the actual product and concentration. Do not rely on memory for a new chemical.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
