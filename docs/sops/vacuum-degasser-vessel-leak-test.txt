Perform vessel leak testing only when the vessel is cool, empty, isolated, and approved for vacuum. Confirm doors, seals, sampling ports, valves, and instrumentation are secure. Pull vacuum in stages and observe the decay rate. Do not enter or open a vessel under vacuum. Record starting pressure, decay, hold time, valve position, and leak location if found. A small leak at a sample port can create a large process delay and oxygen ingress. Repair and retest before accepting the vessel.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
