Before checking crop-shear blade gap, isolate the shear drive and hydraulic pressure, secure the upper blade, and verify the strip path is empty. Inspect blade condition, clamping bolts, guide plates, and blade identification. Use the approved gauge and measurement points; do not place fingers between blades. A small gap error can produce a ragged head, excessive force, or a strip tail that catches downstream equipment. Check the shear zero and stroke feedback after the mechanical measurement. If the blades are chipped or rounded, record the defect and obtain the correct replacement rather than compensating with pressure. Run an unloaded cycle only after guards and interlocks are restored. Record gap at each side, blade number, hydraulic pressure, stroke time, and test result. Experienced operators look for a difference between operator side and drive side because it indicates alignment or clamping trouble. The shear must be proven in low-speed operation before normal threading.
