Before opening the tap hole, inspect the main runner, skimmer, iron trough, slag runner, ladle runner, and emergency diversion path. Refractory must be dry, continuous, and free from loose pieces. Check that the tap-hole drill, mud gun, oxygen lance, and cast-house crane are available and tested. A runner that looks hard on top may still be soft underneath after a rain shower.

Confirm the planned cast duration, expected iron volume, ladle position, and slag practice with the furnace controller. Keep people out of the runner splash zone and do not stand in line with a suspected crack. Remove water hoses and wet scrap from the cast-house floor before opening.

Start the cast with the approved drill setting and watch the first flow. If the hole opens suddenly, step back and control the stream from the safe position. Do not put an oxygen lance into an unstable opening without authorization. Check runner temperature, flow direction, slag carryover, and ladle level throughout the cast.

After closing, inspect for breakout, erosion, hanging skull, and water ingress. Record cast start and end times, hole length, flow behavior, runner condition, and any abnormal slag. The next cast is often decided by what was left after the last one.
