Before charging alloy through a treatment hopper, verify material identity, bin number, weighing zero, gate movement, vibrator condition, and pneumatic conveying line. Confirm the addition plan against heat number, grade, and target chemistry. A similar-looking bag in the wrong bin is a quality event, not a housekeeping issue. Run the hopper interlock test without exposing anyone to the discharge. Check that the receiver has capacity and that dust extraction is working. During addition, compare indicated mass with the planned quantity and watch for bridging, unexpected flow, or a blocked line. If the weight stops changing while the gate is open, stop the sequence and investigate; do not keep vibrating indefinitely. Record batch, mass, start and end time, feeder correction, and operator initials. Moist alloy can bridge and then release as a slug, causing chemistry overshoot. Keep the hopper closed and dry between heats. Any manual addition must be double-checked and entered immediately so traceability is not lost.
