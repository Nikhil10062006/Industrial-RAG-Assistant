Before a ladle, tundish, or hot-slag pot moves, inspect the route for barricades, people, vehicles, water, obstructions, lighting, and escape paths. One signalman controls the movement. Stop if the route changes or communication is lost. Record route, load, crane, barriers, and clearance. A route that was safe ten minutes ago can be blocked by a vehicle or maintenance team. Never assume a horn is enough.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
