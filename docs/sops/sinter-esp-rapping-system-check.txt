Before testing an electrostatic precipitator rapping system, confirm process state, electrical isolation, hopper level, rapping sequence, and safe access. Do not open an ESP or enter a hopper without the stored-energy, confined-space, and dust controls. Check rapper motor, hammer, linkage, limit switch, and control timing from the approved interface. A rapping failure can increase dust emissions and hopper buildup even when fan current is normal. Record field device, sequence, vibration, hopper level, and test result. If the ESP is energized, maintain all electrical boundaries; residual charge can remain after shutdown. Do not strike the casing manually to imitate rapping. Repeated high differential pressure may be caused by dust discharge, gas temperature, moisture, or rapper timing. Coordinate any test with environmental and production control so the emission window is known.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
