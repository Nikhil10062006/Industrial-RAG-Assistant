Before using a rescue tripod, inspect legs, pins, head, winch, cable, retrieval line, anchor, label, and last inspection. Set it on stable ground and secure it against movement. Perform a function check before entry. Record tripod, space, entrant, attendant, gas test, and rescue arrangement. A tripod is useful only when positioned over the actual opening and connected to compatible retrieval equipment. Do not improvise a rescue with a crane.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
