Before greasing a gear coupling, isolate the equipment, confirm coupling type, grease specification, plugs, guard, and alignment history. Remove the correct plug and add only the specified quantity. Excess grease can cause churning and seal failure; too little causes tooth wear. Record grease, amount, coupling temperature, and leakage. Do not remove a plug from a rotating coupling. After restart, inspect vibration and temperature through a load cycle.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
