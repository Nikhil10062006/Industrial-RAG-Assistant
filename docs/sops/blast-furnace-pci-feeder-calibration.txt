Before calibrating a pulverized-coal feeder, verify the line is isolated from furnace injection or placed in the approved test condition. Confirm bin level, feeder identity, nitrogen or carrier-gas status, weighing system zero, screw or belt condition, and dust-extraction readiness. Run the feeder empty only where the procedure permits and ensure no person is exposed to the discharge. Collect a timed quantity using the approved method and compare actual mass with indicated rate. Check for material buildup on load cells, touching skirts, worn flights, and air leakage that can disturb the weighing signal. Never open a coal feeder or bin without dust, fire, and confined-space controls. If the rate is unstable, check bridging and moisture before changing calibration constants. Record test time, material, actual mass, indicated mass, carrier-gas pressure, and calibration result. A small feeder bias affects furnace heat balance over many hours. Operators often know that a feeder which becomes accurate after tapping the casing is bridged, not miscalibrated; the casing must not be struck while energized.
