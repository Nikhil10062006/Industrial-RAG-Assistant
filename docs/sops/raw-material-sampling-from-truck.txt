Before sampling a truck carrying ore, coal, limestone, or scrap, confirm vehicle brakes, wheel chocks, loading status, material identity, sampling tool, and safe access. Do not climb onto a moving or unstable load. Watch for voids, wet pockets, oversized pieces, and material that can slide. Use the approved number and pattern of increments so the sample represents the load. Keep clear of tipping equipment and do not stand between truck and bay wall. Label the sample with supplier, truck, batch, time, material, and sampler. A wet surface does not mean the whole load is wet; sampling bias can affect process chemistry and moisture control. If contamination, foreign material, or unusual odor is found, quarantine the load and notify quality or procurement. Never take a sample from a prohibited area or while the truck is being repositioned.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
