Survey steam traps from a safe distance using temperature, ultrasonic, or approved comparison methods. Do not touch traps or insulation. Identify failed-open, failed-closed, bypassed, and inaccessible traps. Record trap tag, upstream and downstream condition, temperature, sound, and action. A trap that passes steam can waste energy and overload condensate return; a failed-closed trap can create water hammer. Repair according to pressure and temperature isolation requirements.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
