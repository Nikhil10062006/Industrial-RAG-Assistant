Before preparing an inclusion-rating sample, verify heat identity, orientation, cut location, saw condition, cooling method, and surface-cleaning procedure. Avoid overheating the sample or smearing inclusions during grinding and polishing. Use the approved abrasive sequence and clean equipment between heats. Mark rolling direction and surface orientation clearly. Inspect under the specified magnification and lighting, and record fields examined rather than selecting only clean areas. A sample from the wrong location can produce a technically correct rating that does not represent the product. Record sample ID, section, preparation method, polish condition, operator, and result. If the surface is torn, contaminated, or overetched, reject the preparation and repeat it. Keep samples and records separated by heat; similar-looking pieces are not proof of identity.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
