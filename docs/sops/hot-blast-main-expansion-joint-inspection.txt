Inspect an expansion joint only from the approved safe location and temperature condition. Look for bulging, cracks, hot spots, refractory loss, loose insulation, and unusual movement during stove changeover. Do not touch the bellows or remove insulation on a hot main. Compare movement with the expected thermal pattern and check nearby supports. Record joint number, temperature, visual condition, stove state, and any gas readings. A small hot spot can signal internal refractory loss or a leak path. Escalate before the joint fails under pressure.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
