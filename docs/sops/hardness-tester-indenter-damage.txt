Before hardness testing, inspect indenter, anvil, load, calibration block, surface finish, and test spacing. A chipped indenter can produce a repeatable but wrong value. Clean the surface and support the sample correctly. Record instrument, indenter, block result, load, sample, and operator. If a check block is outside tolerance, stop testing and review samples since the last valid check. Do not polish away an abnormal result without recording the original condition.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
