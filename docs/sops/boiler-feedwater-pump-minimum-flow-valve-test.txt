Before testing a boiler feedwater pump minimum-flow valve, confirm pump status, discharge pressure, return path, valve tag, and safe access. The valve protects the pump when main flow is low, so do not isolate or block the return line during the test. Use the approved manual or automatic test sequence and watch flow, pressure, motor current, bearing temperature, and vibration. If the valve fails to open, stop the pump or restore flow according to the boiler procedure; do not continue against a closed discharge. Record opening pressure, return flow, valve feedback, test duration, and any leakage. A minimum-flow valve can look healthy because it moves, but still pass insufficient flow due to a blocked orifice or wrong trim. Verify the actual return flow where possible. The test must be coordinated with boiler demand because a sudden return-flow change can disturb the feedwater system.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
