Before starting a shiploader or port conveyor, verify boom position, berth clearance, belt route, transfer chutes, emergency stops, pull cords, dust suppression, and communication with vessel and shore teams. Run the conveyor empty where required and confirm material flow switches. During loading, monitor belt tracking, motor current, chute blockage, dust, and ship trim. If the conveyor stops, do not reset until the cause is known and the transfer points are clear. A blocked chute can release a large slug when the belt restarts. Record cargo, belt, berth, rate, alarms, weather, and any stop. Keep people away from the boom and suspended structures. Wind and rain can change dust and material behavior. Emergency stops must be tested without creating a live-load hazard.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
