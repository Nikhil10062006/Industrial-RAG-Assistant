Check the length counter against a certified reference or measured strip before release. Verify encoder circumference, wheel pressure, slip, speed, and reset sequence. Record counter, reference length, error, strip condition, and correction. A counter may be accurate on a dry strip and slip on oil or coolant. Hold material if length affects customer order or mass balance. Calibration must include the actual operating condition where possible.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
