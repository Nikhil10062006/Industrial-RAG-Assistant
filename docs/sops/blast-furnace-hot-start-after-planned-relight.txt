A hot start is not the same as a cold start. Before opening the tuyere line, confirm that the furnace has been declared gas-free where required and that the cast house, stoves, dust catcher, and top-pressure system are manned. Check cooling-water flow on every tuyere and stave circuit. A low-flow trip that is bypassed for “just five minutes” has caused more trouble than the original delay.

Bring the stoves to stable dome temperature, normally above 1,000°C, and line up the hot-blast valves one at a time. Hold hot-blast pressure at the approved low-start value, typically 0.6–0.8 bar(g), until the operator confirms even tuyere flame. Do not chase a single dark tuyere by increasing total blast; first check its tuyere camera, tuyere cooler flow, and local coal or oil lance condition.

Keep oxygen enrichment at zero during the first stable period unless the furnace manager authorizes it. Record top pressure, wind volume, blast temperature, furnace differential pressure, and stock-line movement every 15 minutes for the first hour. If top pressure oscillates or burden descent stops, hold the rate and call the furnace controller. A quiet-looking furnace can be hanging internally.
