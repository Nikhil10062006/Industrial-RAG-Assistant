Before inserting a dummy-bar nose, check centering, latch, nose shape, links, and mould alignment. Use the approved guide and slow jog. Stop for rubbing, asymmetric load, or a nose that does not seat. Do not hammer or force the bar into the mould. Record bar ID, nose condition, alignment, load response, and acceptance. A small offset can damage mould copper or foot rolls during threading. Inspect the first support segment after the bar passes.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
