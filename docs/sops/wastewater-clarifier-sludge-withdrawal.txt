Before withdrawing sludge from a clarifier, check clarifier level, rake-drive torque, sludge blanket depth, pump readiness, discharge route, and polymer status where applicable. Confirm the receiving tank has capacity and that the line is not blocked.

Start the sludge pump at low rate and watch suction pressure, discharge pressure, motor current, and blanket response. If pressure rises with no flow, stop and investigate a blocked line or closed valve. Do not keep running against a blocked discharge; the pump and pipe can fail.

Avoid sudden high-rate withdrawal because it can disturb the blanket and carry solids into the treated-water outlet. If the rake torque alarm rises, stop the withdrawal and follow the clarifier recovery procedure. Do not enter the tank or remove a cover to inspect it.

Record blanket depth, withdrawal rate, polymer setting, pressure, and final water clarity. A recurring high blanket may be caused by upstream chemistry or return flow, not only by a weak sludge pump. Note what changed after every adjustment.
