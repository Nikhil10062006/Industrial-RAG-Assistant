Before pressure testing a vessel, pipe, hose, or heat exchanger, confirm test medium, pressure, duration, boundary, relief protection, gauge range, calibrated reference, and exclusion zone. Water testing is preferred where feasible; compressed gas tests require a higher hazard review. Remove or protect instruments and components not rated for the test. Barricade the area and keep people away from flanges, caps, plugs, and temporary blinds. Raise pressure gradually while watching for movement, leaks, and gauge agreement. Never tighten a fitting or approach a leak while the system is pressurized. Depressurize remotely or from the approved safe point before inspection. Record test pressure, medium, temperature, gauge IDs, hold time, results, and sign-off. A successful low-pressure soap test does not prove a high-pressure assembly is safe. Test boundaries must be physically verified in the field.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
