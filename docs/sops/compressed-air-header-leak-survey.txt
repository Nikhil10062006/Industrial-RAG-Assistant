Conduct a plant air-leak survey during a stable non-production period. Use the approved detector and mark each leak with location, pressure, service, severity, and owner. Do not listen near a high-pressure jet with bare ears or hands. Check hoses, couplings, drains, regulators, unused branches, and instrument cabinets. Record compressor load before and after repair. A leak that is small near one tool may be large over a month. Do not reduce header pressure below process need to hide leakage. Close temporary air users after the survey.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
