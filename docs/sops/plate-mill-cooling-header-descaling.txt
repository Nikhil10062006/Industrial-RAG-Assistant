Before descaling a plate-mill cooling header, isolate pressure, drain safely, confirm nozzle map, and protect nearby electrical and walking areas. Use the approved chemical or mechanical method and compatible materials. Record header, nozzle count, pressure, flow, deposits, and waste handling. After cleaning, verify spray pattern and support condition. Do not open a high-pressure header because the gauge appears zero; trapped water may remain. Uneven cooling affects flatness and mechanical properties.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
