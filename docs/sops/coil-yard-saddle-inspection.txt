Before placing a coil on a saddle, inspect saddle width, chocks, welds, base, restraint, floor condition, and identification. Remove scale, water, oil, and loose objects that could cause the coil to move. Match coil weight, width, and diameter to the saddle rating. The crane operator and signalman must agree on the approach and keep people out of the roll path. Lower slowly and confirm both sides are seated before releasing the hook. Record saddle number, coil number, condition, and any defect. A cracked weld or missing chock can remain unnoticed until the coil is loaded. During monsoon, check drainage and corrosion around saddles. Do not store a coil on an improvised timber or an unmarked floor position.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
