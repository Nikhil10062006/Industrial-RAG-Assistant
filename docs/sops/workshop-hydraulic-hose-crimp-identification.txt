Before installing a replacement hydraulic hose, verify pressure rating, temperature, fluid compatibility, hose size, length, bend radius, end fittings, crimp specification, and cleanliness. Match the hose assembly to the equipment tag and old routing, but do not copy a damaged or twisted installation. Cap and clean open lines, and keep dirt out of the circuit. After installation, check that the hose is not rubbing, kinked, stretched, or exposed to hot metal. Pressurize gradually from a protected position and inspect for leaks using cardboard or a mirror, never a hand. Record hose number, crimp batch, pressure test, routing, and replacement reason. A pinhole hydraulic jet can inject fluid through skin without looking dramatic. Use clamps and guards as designed. Recheck after the first heat cycle because hose movement changes with pressure and temperature.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
