Before releasing a billet bundle, inspect strap count, tension, seal, corner protection, bundle weight, and lift points. The bundle must be stable for crane, truck, or rail movement. Record billet IDs, bundle number, strap type, weight, and inspection. Do not stand at the bundle end while tension is applied. A loose strap can allow billets to roll; an over-tight strap can cut into hot material or fail after cooling. Recheck after the first handling move.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
