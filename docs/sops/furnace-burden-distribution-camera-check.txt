Before a burden-distribution camera inspection, confirm top equipment is in the approved state, gas testing is complete, purge air is available, and access barriers are intact. Compare the image with stockline, hopper weight, chute angle, and recent charging sequence. A blurred image may be a dirty window, low purge, condensation, or a real change in material flow. Do not open a top cover or enter the platform to improve the view without the permit and isolation. Clean the window only with the approved method. Record camera tag, image quality, purge pressure, burden condition, and any unusual distribution. A camera is evidence, not permission to ignore a level or pressure alarm.  Record the equipment tag, readings with units, time, field verifier, and handover status. If the field condition differs from the screen, drawing, or job card, stop and resolve the difference before continuing. A clean closeout includes guards, barriers, drains, tools, temporary controls, and the exact condition for return to service. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
