Before changing a mud-gun nozzle, confirm the gun is parked, hydraulic pressure is isolated, residual mud pressure is released, and the tap-hole area is declared safe. The gun may look empty while hot mud remains inside the barrel. Wear the specified heat and face protection and use the designated tools.

Inspect the new nozzle for damage, correct size, and clean threads or seating surfaces. Do not install a nozzle with a chipped refractory tip. Apply only the approved compound; excess material can enter the mud barrel and alter the extrusion pressure.

After fitting, jog the gun without entering the danger zone and check the pressure response. If pressure rises unusually fast, stop. A blocked nozzle, hardened mud, or incorrect nozzle length may be present. Do not solve a pressure alarm by increasing hydraulic pressure beyond the approved limit.

Before returning the gun to service, confirm the parked position, limit switches, hydraulic leakage, and communication with the cast-house operator. Record nozzle size, change reason, pressure during test, and the next planned inspection. Veteran operators know that a nozzle that works once can still fail when the gun is needed urgently.
