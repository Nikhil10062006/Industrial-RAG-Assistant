Before printing heat or coil labels, verify printer identity, template revision, barcode readability, ink or ribbon condition, label stock, and data source. Print a test label and scan it with the approved reader. Confirm that heat, coil, grade, size, weight, and destination match the production record. If the printer fails, use the controlled manual label process; do not create a handwritten substitute without authorization. Record printer tag, template, test result, rejected labels, and affected material. A barcode that scans but contains the wrong heat is worse than a missing barcode because it creates false confidence. Keep rejected labels destroyed or controlled so they cannot be reused. Reconcile printed quantity with dispatched quantity at handover.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
