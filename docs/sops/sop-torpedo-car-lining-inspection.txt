Procedure for the thermographic inspection of 300-ton Torpedo Car shells. The internal refractory bricks erode over time due to the corrosive nature of the blast furnace slag and thermal cycling. If the bricks wear too thin, the molten iron will melt through the steel outer shell.
At the start of every shift, the designated inspector must scan every torpedo car waiting at the deslagging station using the FLIR thermal imaging camera. Baseline external shell temperature is normally 150°C to 220°C.
If any localized "hot spot" exceeds 320°C, flag the car immediately. Paint a large white 'X' on the hot spot using temperature-resistant chalk. The car is permitted to complete its current discharge into the BOF, but it must be immediately locked out of the rotation and sent to the refractory relining shop. If a spot exceeds 400°C, do not move the car. Clear the area and call the shift manager; a burn-through is imminent.
