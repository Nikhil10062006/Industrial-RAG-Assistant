Before visual inspection, check lamp intensity, color temperature, angle, cleanliness, backup power, and glare control. The inspection surface must be free of oil, dust, and loose scale that could hide or imitate defects. Confirm the coil or plate identity and inspection direction. A lamp with one failed tube can make one side look cleaner than the other. Record lamp ID, condition, illumination check, inspector, material, and defect findings. If lighting changes during a coil, mark the affected section and review it under accepted conditions. Do not rely on a phone flashlight for release decisions. Veteran inspectors know that defect visibility depends on angle and contrast, not only brightness. Correct the inspection environment before changing product disposition.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
