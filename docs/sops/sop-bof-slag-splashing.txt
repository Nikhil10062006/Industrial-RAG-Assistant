Execution of the Slag Splashing sequence in the Basic Oxygen Furnace (BOF) immediately after tapping a heat. This process uses high-pressure nitrogen to blast residual liquid slag onto the refractory walls, freezing it into a protective coating that significantly extends vessel life.
After the steel is tapped, the pulpit operator must verify the remaining slag is properly conditioned (thick and creamy, not watery). If it is too fluid, add 500 kg of dolomitic lime to chill it. Lower the main oxygen lance to a height of 1.5 meters above the bath—do NOT submerge the lance tip into the slag.
Switch the lance supply from Oxygen to Nitrogen. Ramp the nitrogen flow rapidly to 12,000 Nm3/hr. You will hear a massive roar as the gas hits the slag pool. The sequence must run for exactly 3 minutes. Slowly raise the lance while blowing to coat the upper cone of the vessel. Monitor the lance cooling water return temperature; if it exceeds 65°C, abort the splash immediately and withdraw the lance to prevent melting the copper tip.
