At the end of a maintenance job, account for tools, fasteners, rags, test leads, temporary supports, blinds, and lifting accessories before the equipment is restored. Use the job tool list and inspect the work area, guards, cabinets, drain trays, and adjacent platforms. Do not assume a missing small item is harmless around a rotating, hot, or high-pressure machine. Check inside accessible covers only after isolation remains valid. Remove temporary jumpers and test instruments or record them clearly if they must remain. Confirm all guards, handrails, covers, and interlocks are restored. Record the final tool count, outstanding temporary measures, and person who accepted the area. Foreign-object damage often begins with a routine closeout that was rushed. A clean handback protects the next operator and proves the repair was completed, not merely the component replacement.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
