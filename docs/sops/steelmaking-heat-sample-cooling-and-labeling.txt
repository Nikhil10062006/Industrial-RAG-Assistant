After taking a steel sample, place it in the designated cooling fixture away from water, wet floors, and moving traffic. Confirm heat number, ladle number, sample position, grade, and time before the sample leaves the operator’s hand. Do not mark a hot sample by leaning over it or using an unapproved tool. The sample label must match the heat record and the physical mark. If the sample is chipped, contaminated with slag, or taken late, record that condition rather than hiding it. Allow adequate cooling before machining or testing; forced cooling can alter some observations and creates a splash hazard. Keep sample tongs and racks clean so material from one heat is not transferred to another. Record sampler, location, temperature if required, and any deviation. A laboratory result is only as reliable as the chain of identity and handling. If identity is uncertain, quarantine the sample and notify quality instead of guessing the heat.
