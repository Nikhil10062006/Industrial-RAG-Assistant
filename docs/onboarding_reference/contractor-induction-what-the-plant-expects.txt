Every contractor must know the site emergency number, muster point, access restrictions, permit process, PPE zones, traffic routes, and who controls the equipment. General induction does not replace a task briefing at the work location.

Before starting, verify the job scope, drawing revision, isolation boundary, tools, lifting plan, gas test, fall protection, and rescue method. If the field condition differs from the permit, stop and ask for revalidation. Do not make a small scope change without checking whether the hazard assessment changes.

Keep tools and materials within the approved area. Maintain housekeeping because scale, oil, cables, and hoses create trip and fire hazards. Report near misses, damaged barriers, missing tags, and unexpected pressure or heat without fear of blame.

Contractors must not operate plant equipment, defeat interlocks, remove guards, or enter another area because access is convenient. At completion, remove tools, close openings, restore guards, and participate in handover. The plant owns the process hazard even when a contractor performs the work, so coordination must be explicit.
