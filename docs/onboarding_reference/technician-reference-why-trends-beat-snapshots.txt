A single value answers “what was happening at one moment.” A trend helps answer “what changed, how fast, and after which action.” Use trends for pressure, temperature, vibration, current, flow, level, dew point, and quality measurements whenever the system provides them.

Mark important events on the trend: start, stop, valve movement, material change, alarm, reset, maintenance, weather change, and operator adjustment. Without event timing, a curve is only a picture.

Compare related variables. Rising pressure with falling flow suggests restriction; rising motor current with falling flow suggests a mechanical or process load; changing signal quality without process movement suggests an instrument issue. Correlation does not prove cause, but it narrows the next safe check.

Save the trend before resetting or power-cycling equipment. Include units, time zone, tag, and sample interval in the record. A screenshot without a timestamp or scale is weak evidence. Trends turn individual experience into a repeatable troubleshooting method.
