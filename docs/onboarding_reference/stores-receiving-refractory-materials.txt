When refractory arrives, verify product, grade, batch, quantity, packaging, manufacturing date, and storage instructions against the purchase and repair plan. Inspect bags, bricks, castables, boards, and shapes for water damage, torn packaging, cracks, or contamination.

Store materials off the floor, under cover, and within the temperature and humidity limits specified by the supplier. Keep different grades separated. Do not use old material because it is already open unless the batch and shelf-life check allow it.

For castables and ramming mixes, record batch number and expiry at issue. For bricks and precast shapes, protect edges and maintain orientation. Refractory that has absorbed rain may look normal but fail during dry-out or service.

Quarantine damaged or expired material. Do not mix batches without technical approval. The store record should connect material to ladle, furnace, tundish, runner, or repair location. Good refractory control begins before the material reaches the hot area.
