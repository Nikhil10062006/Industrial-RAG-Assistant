A defect tag should identify the equipment, exact location, observed condition, time, operating state, and immediate control. Write “leak at pump P-204 mechanical seal, 3 drops per minute at 08:40, pump running at 75%, drip tray half full” rather than “pump leaking.”

Include whether the defect is safe to operate, what limit applies, and who was informed. Do not write a repair diagnosis unless it was confirmed. A tag should help the next person find the same condition without guessing.

Use durable labels and place them where they can be read without entering danger. Do not attach a tag to a moving part, hot surface, electrical conductor, or pressurized hose. Update or remove the tag only through the approved process.

Photographs help when the location is crowded, but they do not replace the equipment number and written description. A good defect tag is a small piece of operational memory that remains after the person who noticed the problem has gone home.
