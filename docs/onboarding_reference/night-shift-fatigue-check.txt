At night handover, check whether people are fit for the task, especially crane operation, gas testing, confined-space work, driving, and maintenance on energized or hot equipment. Fatigue signs include slow response, repeated mistakes, poor radio read-back, irritability, and difficulty recalling the last step. Use planned breaks, hydration, lighting, and task rotation. Do not shame a worker for reporting fatigue; reassign or pause the task through the supervisor. Record any change that affects the permit or staffing. A short job at 03:00 may have higher risk than a long job at 10:00. Supervisors should ask specific questions rather than rely on “all okay.” Fatigue control is part of human reliability and should be included in toolbox talks for night work.

Record the equipment tag, operating condition, readings with units, and person who accepted the result. Compare the actual field condition with the control-room indication and the approved drawing or job card. If any value is outside the normal band, hold the task and obtain the responsible specialist rather than making an undocumented adjustment. At handover, state what was checked, what was changed, what remains open, and the exact condition that would require a stop.
