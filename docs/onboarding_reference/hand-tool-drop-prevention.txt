When working above equipment, secure tools with approved lanyards or use a tool bag. Do not place spanners, phones, electrodes, or bolts on ledges, panels, or crane beams. Establish a lower exclusion zone and account for tools at closeout. Record dropped-object near misses and correct the work method. A small tool can damage a belt, create an electrical short, or injure someone below. Tool control is part of the permit.  Write the tag, reading, unit, time, action, and person accepting the condition. A new technician should know what is normal, what is unsafe, what can be checked without entry, and when to call the responsible specialist. Knowledge is useful when it makes the next safe decision easier, not when it encourages an undocumented shortcut. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
