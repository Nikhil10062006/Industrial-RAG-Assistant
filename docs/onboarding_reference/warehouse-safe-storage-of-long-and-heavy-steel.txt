Store plates, bars, coils, billets, pipes, and refractory materials on stable supports designed for the load. Confirm the floor capacity, chock condition, stack height, access route, and lifting point. Do not add a layer to a stack because it “looks stable.”

Keep different grades, sizes, heat numbers, and customer orders separated and identified. Wet or oily material can move differently from dry material. Coils require correct saddles and chocks; a coil that rolls a few centimetres can crush a person or vehicle.

Use approved lifting equipment and inspect slings, magnets, hooks, and clamps before use. Stand clear of suspended loads and do not guide with hands near pinch points. Keep pedestrians out of the lift path.

During rain, check water accumulation, slippery surfaces, rust, labels, and drainage. At handover, report shifted stacks, damaged chocks, missing tags, and blocked routes. Storage is part of traceability and safety, not merely a place to park material.
