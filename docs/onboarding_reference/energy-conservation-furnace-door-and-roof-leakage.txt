Furnace leakage wastes fuel and exposes people to heat, flame, and combustion products. During rounds, look for glowing gaps, flame roll-out, positive pressure puffs, damaged seals, and unusual shell temperature. Do not stand directly in front of a door or peep sight to inspect it.

Compare furnace pressure, oxygen, fuel flow, and zone temperature with the normal condition. A door that leaks may also upset combustion and distort pyrometer readings. Report the exact door, side, time, and process state.

Repairs require the approved temperature, access, and isolation controls. Do not patch a hot door with unapproved material or adjust dampers blindly. A small positive-pressure event can spread hot gas and sparks into a work area.

Record before-and-after fuel flow and pressure where available. Energy work is not separate from safety; a sealed furnace is usually more stable and less hazardous. If a leak appears only during charging or discharging, inspect the sequence and pressure response rather than only the refractory.
