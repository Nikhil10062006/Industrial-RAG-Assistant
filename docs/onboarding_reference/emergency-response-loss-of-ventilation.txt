Ventilation loss can create heat, gas, fume, dust, or oxygen-deficiency hazards even when process alarms remain normal. Stop non-essential work and move out of affected areas if the risk assessment requires. Do not enter a hot or gas-prone area to inspect a fan without authorization.

Identify whether the problem is fan power, damper position, filter blockage, control signal, belt failure, or duct damage. Check local and remote indication together. A fan motor may run while the belt, impeller, or damper has failed.

Suspend hot work, chemical transfer, gas sampling, and confined-space entry until ventilation is proven. Gas test where required. Do not open doors or start unapproved fans if that could move contaminants into occupied areas.

After repair, verify airflow, direction, pressure, temperature, gas or dust readings, and alarm response. Record the duration and areas affected. Ventilation is a process barrier; its failure should be treated with the same seriousness as a failed pump or valve.
