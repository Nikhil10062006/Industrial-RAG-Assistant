When writing a new plant-floor knowledge entry, state the equipment and operating context first. Describe the symptom in plain language, then the checks that were safe, the evidence found, the correction, and the condition for return to service. Include numbers with units, tags, dates, and the author’s role where useful. Separate confirmed cause from suspicion. Write the veteran clue, but also write the limit: an alarm may be commonly false under one condition and still require full response under another. Do not record bypasses, shortcuts, or undocumented limits as acceptable practice. Link the entry to the formal SOP, permit, drawing, or OEM instruction when those exist. Review old notes after equipment or software changes. The goal is not to sound experienced; it is to help the next technician make a safer, faster, evidence-based decision without replacing the approved control system
