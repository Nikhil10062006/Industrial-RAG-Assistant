New technicians should walk the plant map with an experienced person and mark hot-metal routes, gas zones, crane bays, vehicle crossings, emergency exits, muster points, eyewash stations, fire equipment, medical points, and safe observation areas. The shortest route is not always the safest route.

Learn which paths close during casting, ladle transfer, slag movement, charging, crane operation, or gas work. Temporary barricades are meaningful; do not cross because no one is standing there. If a route is blocked, use the approved alternative and report the obstruction.

Mark locations where phones or radios do not work well, where floor levels change, where steam or gas can collect, and where lighting is poor. A map should show operational reality, not only buildings.

Update the map when equipment, traffic, barriers, or emergency arrangements change. During an alarm, move by the known safe route and follow the area controller. Orientation is a practical control against getting lost during a noisy, smoky, or confusing event.
