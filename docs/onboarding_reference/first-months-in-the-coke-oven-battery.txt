Learn the battery layout, pusher and charging-car routes, door numbers, gas lines, valve pits, regenerator access, standpipes, and muster points. Carry the approved gas detector where required and know what the readings mean. Do not enter pits or enclosed areas without the permit and test.

During rounds, observe door sealing, smoke, flame, heating pattern, standpipe condition, battery pressure, and equipment travel. A small change in door leakage or oven pressure may be the first sign of a larger issue. Report location and direction, not only “smoke seen.”

Stay clear of moving pusher machines and charging equipment. Make eye contact with operators and use the designated crossings. Never stand between a door and frame or near a moving charging-car bucket.

At handover, record oven number, door condition, gas changes, heating abnormalities, pushing delays, and any repair in progress. The battery has many repeated units, so precise numbering matters. A veteran may recognize a sound or smell, but you should connect it to the correct oven and process variable.
