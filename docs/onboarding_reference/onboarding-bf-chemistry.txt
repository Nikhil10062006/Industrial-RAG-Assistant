Blast Furnace Chemistry - The Absolute Basics
The Blast Furnace is a massive chemical reactor. We drop iron ore (Fe2O3), coke (Carbon), and limestone (CaCO3) in the top, and blow hot air (Oxygen) in the bottom.

The Oxygen burns the Carbon to make Carbon Monoxide (CO) gas and extreme heat (2000°C).

The hot CO gas rises up through the furnace.

The CO gas literally strips the oxygen atoms away from the iron ore, leaving pure liquid iron (Fe) to pool at the bottom.

The limestone melts and acts like a sponge, absorbing the dirt and impurities from the ore to create liquid slag, which floats on top of the iron.
Your goal is to keep the furnace hot and permeable. If the gas can't flow up smoothly, the reaction stops, the furnace goes cold, and the iron freezes solid.
