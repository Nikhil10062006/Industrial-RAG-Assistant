Before publishing a plant-floor knowledge entry, verify equipment tag, category, author or source, date, process area, symptom or task, safe boundary, evidence, correction, and current approval status. Separate practical hints from mandatory controls. Include units, thresholds, alarm context, and the condition that requires escalation. Mark fictional or training values clearly and never present them as site limits. Review entries after equipment, software, layout, or procedure changes. A useful note tells a fresher what to observe and what not to do, while preserving permits, lockout, PPE, quality holds, and emergency response. The repository should have an owner and review date; otherwise old tribal knowledge can become a hidden risk. Ask a technician unfamiliar with the original event to read the entry. If the note cannot be understood or safely applied without the author, rewrite it before indexing.  Write the tag, reading, unit, time, action, and person accepting the condition. A new technician should know what is normal, what is unsafe, what can be checked without entry, and when to call the responsible specialist. Knowledge is useful when it makes the next safe decision easier, not when it encourages an undocumented shortcut. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
