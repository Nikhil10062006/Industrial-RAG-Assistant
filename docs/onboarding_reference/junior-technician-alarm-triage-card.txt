When an alarm appears, first protect people. Stop the job or equipment if there is immediate danger, move to a safe position, and raise the call. Then identify the equipment, alarm text, time, and process state. Do not acknowledge a banner and walk away without checking whether the field condition is still present.

Second, separate stop-now alarms from investigate-now alarms using the approved alarm philosophy. Gas, fire, breakout, overspeed, loss of cooling, and unexpected movement are never routine. A signal-quality alarm may be less urgent, but it can hide a dangerous process condition, so confirm with an independent indication.

Third, preserve evidence. Save the trend, note the sequence of alarms, and ask what changed immediately before the first one. Check local gauges only from a safe place. Never open a guard, cabinet, valve pit, or vessel to investigate without the right isolation and permit.

Finally, communicate in facts: “Caster mould level signal quality fell for three seconds during powder addition; manual level steady; purge pressure 3.1 bar(g).” Avoid conclusions such as “sensor bad” until the test supports them. At handover, state what was checked, what was not checked, and what condition requires a stop.
