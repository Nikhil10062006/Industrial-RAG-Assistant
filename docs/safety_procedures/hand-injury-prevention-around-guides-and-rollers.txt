Most hand injuries around rolling equipment happen during small adjustments, cleaning, or attempts to remove a short strip tail. The machine may be inching, but the nip point is still a nip point. Keep hands outside the marked danger zone and use the approved tool for threading, guiding, or clearing.

Before touching a guide, roller, crop shear, or pinch point, stop and isolate the relevant movement. Do not rely on a foot switch being released or on another operator saying “it is off.” Test the isolation and communicate with everyone at the station.

Wear gloves appropriate to the task, but remember that gloves can be caught by rotating equipment. Gloves are for handling material when the equipment is safe, not for reaching into a running line. Remove rings, bracelets, loose sleeves, and other items that can catch.

If a strip edge is sharp, support it with the designed clamp or hook. Never pull a jammed strip by hand. If a guide needs to be nudged, use the jog function only from the approved position with guards and line-of-sight controls in place. Report near misses such as a glove mark, sudden strip movement, or unexpected jog. These are early warnings, not harmless stories.
