Before using a compressed-air tool, inspect hose, coupling, whip check, regulator, trigger, guard, and tool condition. Secure the hose against whipping if a coupling separates. Do not use compressed air to clean clothing, skin, bearings, or electrical panels. Set pressure within the tool rating and wear eye and hearing protection. Keep the hose away from hot work, sharp edges, walkways, and rotating equipment. Close the supply before disconnecting and bleed trapped pressure. Record defective hose, coupling, regulator, or tool and remove it from service. A small coupling failure can turn a hose into a striking object. Do not repair a damaged hose with tape or use a mismatched fitting. At shift end, isolate the branch and drain moisture from the line.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
