If a flange gasket blows or a water jet appears, move away from the line of fire and notify the area controller. Do not approach to close a nearby valve unless the procedure confirms the valve can be operated from a safe location. High-pressure water can penetrate skin and carry scale or chemicals. Keep people and vehicles outside the spray area, isolate electrical equipment if water is reaching it, and control drains to prevent flooding. Record pressure, temperature, fluid, flange location, and people exposed. After pressure is isolated and drained, inspect bolts, gasket, flange faces, pipe supports, and valve position. Do not reuse a damaged gasket or simply tighten bolts while the line is live. A flange may fail because of water hammer, misalignment, thermal movement, wrong gasket, or overpressure. The repair should address the event mechanism, not only the visible leak.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
