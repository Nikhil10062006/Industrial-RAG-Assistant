If a bearing heater exceeds its limit, stop heating, isolate power, and keep the area clear until the bearing temperature is confirmed. Check sensor placement, controller setting, insulation, and heater coil condition. Do not cool a hot bearing with water. Record bearing, heater, temperature, time, sensor, and action. Excess heating can remove lubricant properties, damage seals, or alter fit. A controller reading alone is not enough; use the approved independent measurement.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
