Store gas cylinders upright, secured, capped when not connected, separated by service, and protected from heat, impact, oil, and unauthorized handling. Keep oxygen cylinders away from fuel-gas cylinders by the approved separation or barrier. Do not drag, roll, lift by the cap, or use a cylinder as a support. Check label, valve, regulator, hose, and test date before connection. Leak-test connections with the approved method and close the valve when the cylinder is not in use. Keep cylinders out of escape routes and below crane hooks or hot-work sparks. If a cylinder is damaged, leaking, or overheated, evacuate the immediate area and call the responsible gas team. Record cylinder number, service, location, defect, and supplier status. A small cylinder can become a projectile if unsecured; storage discipline is a workshop control, not a stores preference.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
