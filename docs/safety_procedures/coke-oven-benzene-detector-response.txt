If a benzene or toxic-vapor detector alarms, stop work and move upwind or crosswind to the designated safe area. Do not rely on smell or attempt to identify the leak by walking closer. Notify the gas and environmental controllers with the exact location, detector reading, wind, and work in progress. Keep ignition sources and vehicles out. Gas testing must use the approved instrument and sampling plan, including pits and low points where applicable. Personnel with symptoms such as dizziness, headache, or irritation report to medical services. Do not re-enter until the area is controlled, the source isolated, and clearance documented. Record alarm history, readings, weather, source, exposure list, and corrective action. A detector that clears after ventilation does not prove the source is repaired. Toxic-vapor response requires both immediate protection and a traceable investigation.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
