A flame scanner cooling-air alarm requires controlled burner action and inspection from outside the furnace. Confirm air pressure, filter, hose, and scanner temperature. Do not open the sight port while firing or bypass flame protection. Record burner, air pressure, flame status, furnace load, and response. Loss of cooling can damage the scanner and create false flame signals. Restore cooling and prove flame supervision before normal firing.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
