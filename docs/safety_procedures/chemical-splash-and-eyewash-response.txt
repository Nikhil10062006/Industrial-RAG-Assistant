If acid, caustic, inhibitor, or treatment chemical contacts skin or eyes, start flushing immediately at the nearest safety shower or eyewash. Do not wait to identify the chemical or search for a supervisor. Hold eyelids open and remove contaminated clothing while flushing, provided this can be done safely.

Call the site emergency number while flushing or have another person call. Use the safety data sheet to guide medical staff, but do not delay irrigation to retrieve paperwork. Do not neutralize a chemical on the skin with another chemical. Do not apply ointment or cover the area before medical evaluation.

For a spill without exposure, stop the source if safe, keep people away, and use the approved spill kit. Do not wash chemicals into an uncontrolled drain. Ventilation and respiratory controls depend on the substance and the amount released.

Report the exact chemical, concentration if known, body area exposed, duration of flushing, and symptoms. Eyewash nozzles and showers must be tested routinely and kept unobstructed. A station hidden behind pallets is not an available emergency control.
