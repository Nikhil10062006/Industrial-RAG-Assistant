Steel plants contain cranes, forklifts, slag haulers, coil carriers, trucks, and maintenance vehicles with limited visibility. Pedestrians must use marked walkways and make eye contact with the operator before crossing. A horn does not prove the driver has seen you.

Operators must control speed for the surface, load, visibility, and traffic. Reverse movement requires the designated alarm, camera or spotter where specified, and a clear route. Do not walk behind a vehicle waiting for it to move. Keep clear of pinch points between a vehicle, coil, ladle, rail, or wall.

Loads must be secured and within the equipment rating. A forklift carrying a long or wide load may hide a person or obstruction. Stop if the route is blocked or a pedestrian enters the exclusion zone. Never ride on forks, coil cars, crane hooks, or improvised platforms.

At shift handover, report damaged barriers, blind corners, failed alarms, oil on the road, and changed traffic routes. Temporary cones often move; permanent separation is better. Most vehicle near misses begin with a familiar route and a small assumption.
