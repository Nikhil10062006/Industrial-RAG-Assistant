Inspect segment hydraulic hoses for chafe, heat damage, twist, and contact with roll frames. If a hose fails, stop movement, isolate pressure, and keep people clear of the jet. Do not touch a suspected pinhole leak. Replace the hose with the correct pressure and temperature rating, route it with the specified bend radius, and secure it away from moving steel. Record hose ID, failure point, pressure, routing, and test. A hose may fail after repeated thermal cycles even if the outer cover looks intact.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
