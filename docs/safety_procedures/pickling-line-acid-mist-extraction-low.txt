If acid mist extraction falls below the operating condition, stop or reduce chemical exposure according to the line procedure and keep people away from open tanks. Do not open covers to inspect a fan while fumes are visible. Check fan status, damper position, filter differential pressure, duct leakage, and scrubber condition from safe locations. Respiratory protection must match the chemical and site program; a dust mask is not an acid-gas control. If the extraction failure is caused by power loss, keep hot work and ignition sources away from the affected area. Gas or vapor testing may be required before entry. Record fan number, pressure, tank temperature, acid concentration, visible mist, and time to safe condition. A fan motor running does not prove airflow; the belt, impeller, damper, or duct may have failed. Return to production only after airflow and chemical conditions are verified.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
