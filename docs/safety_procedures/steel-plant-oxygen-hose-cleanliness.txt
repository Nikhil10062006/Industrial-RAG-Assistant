Before connecting an oxygen hose, verify oxygen-clean status, cap condition, fittings, regulator rating, and absence of oil or grease. Keep tools and gloves clean. Leak-test with the approved method and open the valve slowly. If contamination is found, quarantine the hose; do not wipe it with an oily rag. Record hose ID, service, inspection, cleaning status, and test. Oxygen-enriched equipment can ignite from a small friction spark or contaminated thread. Cleanliness is a process control, not a cosmetic standard.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
