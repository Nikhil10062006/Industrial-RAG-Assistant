During a fire-pump test, stop or reduce the test if vibration, pressure, leakage, or bearing temperature is abnormal. Keep the discharge route and test header controlled. Record pump, suction pressure, discharge pressure, flow, current, vibration, and duration. A fire pump must not be left impaired without notification and compensating controls. Do not tighten a packing gland or open a guard while running. The purpose of the test is readiness, not simply motor start.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
