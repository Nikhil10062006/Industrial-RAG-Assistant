Oxygen-enriched areas require stricter controls than ordinary gas-service areas because materials ignite more easily and burn more intensely. Keep oil, grease, contaminated gloves, rags, and non-approved tools away from oxygen valves, hoses, manifolds, and equipment. Do not use an oxygen hose for any other gas.

Before maintenance, verify the line identity, isolation, depressurization, and gas-free or oxygen-clear condition according to the permit. Use oxygen-clean components and approved leak-detection methods. Never apply ordinary thread compound or lubricant to oxygen service unless it is explicitly approved.

If an oxygen leak is suspected, stop ignition sources, keep people away, and notify the gas controller. Do not touch a leaking fitting with oily gloves or attempt a quick tightening while the line is pressurized. Ventilation must not direct enriched gas toward hot work, electrical arcing, or occupied areas.

After repair, test the line, remove contamination, and restore the oxygen-clean status. Record the source, isolation, component replaced, test result, and clearance authority. Oxygen itself does not burn, but it turns small ignition sources into severe fires; treat a quiet leak seriously.
