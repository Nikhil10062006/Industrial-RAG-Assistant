If laboratory fume-hood flow is low, stop open chemical work, close containers, and move away from the hood. Check fan, sash, filter, alarm, and duct from outside. Do not continue because the chemical has little smell. Record hood, flow, chemical, work state, and response. A hood protects breathing air only when it has verified capture. Resume after flow is proven and the lab supervisor releases the area.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
