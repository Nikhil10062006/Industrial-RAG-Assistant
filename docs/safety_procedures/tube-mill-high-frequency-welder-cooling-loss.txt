Loss of cooling water to a high-frequency welding unit requires immediate power reduction or shutdown according to the OEM sequence. Keep people away from the torch, coil, and exposed conductors. Verify flow, pressure, inlet temperature, pump status, and leak detection from the safe panel. Do not open the cabinet or touch hoses while the welder remains energized. A small water leak can reach high-voltage components and create both electrical and steam hazards. If the flow alarm is false, prove it with an independent gauge before restoring power. Record unit number, current, cooling flow, temperature, pressure, leak location, and trip time. Check the chiller, strainer, hose, quick coupling, and interlock circuit. Do not defeat the cooling permissive because the weld appears stable; the heat load can damage the resonator or transformer quickly. Restart only after cooling is restored and insulation checks are complete.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
