If a hydraulic hose bursts near a walkway, stop the equipment, isolate pressure, keep people away from the spray and slippery area, and control ignition or hot-surface contact. Never search for a pinhole with a hand. Record fluid, pressure, hose tag, location, equipment state, and exposure. Clean the spill with the approved method after energy is isolated. Replace the hose with correct rating and routing. Inspect adjacent hoses because the same heat or abrasion may affect them.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
