If a capacitor-bank alarm or bulging unit is found, keep people outside the electrical boundary and isolate according to the electrical procedure. Capacitors retain charge after supply is removed. Verify discharge and absence of voltage before opening. Record bank, phase, alarm, temperature, visual condition, and protection status. Do not re-energize repeatedly to see whether the alarm clears. A failed capacitor can rupture or damage switchgear. Check harmonics and ventilation before replacing the unit.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
