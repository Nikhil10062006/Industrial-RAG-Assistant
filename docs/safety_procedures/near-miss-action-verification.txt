Operating area: Environmental controls, emergency response, and onboarding. Use the correct detector, PPE, permit, sample point, and rescue arrangement. A cleared alarm or dry surface does not prove the hazard is gone. A near-miss action was marked complete without field evidence. Reopen the action, identify the missing evidence, and assign a field verifier. The action is considered closed only when the changed barrier works. Record the equipment tag, material or heat identity, time, readings with units, people informed, and the final status. If a temporary control, bypass, manual setting, or alternate route is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not close the event because the alarm cleared or the equipment restarted once. Repeat the relevant test under the same load, speed, temperature, pressure, or material condition that caused the problem. At handover, state what was checked, what remains uncertain, and the exact condition that requires stopping the equipment. Practical plant knowledge is useful when it makes the next safe decision easier without replacing isolation, permit, quality, emergency, or engineering controls.
