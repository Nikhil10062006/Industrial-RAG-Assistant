If a receiver safety valve lifts unexpectedly, keep people clear of the discharge and do not attempt to block or clamp the valve. Notify utilities control and check pressure, compressor status, isolation valves, dryer condition, and downstream demand from safe locations. The discharge can be noisy and may carry condensate or debris. Do not assume the valve is faulty because the pressure later returns to normal. Preserve the pressure trend and inspect the pressure switch, control logic, check valve, and receiver drain. A safety valve that lifts is a protective device and must not be silenced without engineering review. Record set pressure if known, lift time, receiver pressure, compressor state, condensate, and downstream conditions. Before resetting or replacing the valve, ensure the receiver is isolated and depressurized by the approved method. A repeated lift can indicate control failure, blocked outlet, wrong valve setting, or thermal expansion in a trapped section.
