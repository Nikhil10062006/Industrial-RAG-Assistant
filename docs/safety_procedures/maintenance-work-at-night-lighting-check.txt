Before starting night maintenance, inspect illumination at the work face, access route, isolation points, lifting path, and emergency exit. Portable lights must be rated for the area, protected from damage, and routed so they do not create trip hazards. Do not use a phone light as the primary work light near moving equipment, gas, or hot metal. Shadows can hide oil, scale, open drains, missing guards, and misaligned lifting gear. If lighting is inadequate, stop and arrange fixed or approved temporary lighting. Record the location, defect, and temporary control. Night work also requires clear radio communication and a buddy or attendant where the task risk demands it. A job that is safe in daylight can be unsafe when the person cannot see the isolation tag or the edge of a platform. Restore lighting before handing equipment back.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
