Water and molten metal must be treated as an explosive combination. Keep hoses, wet scrap, wet tools, rainwater, and unverified refractory away from ladles, tundishes, moulds, runners, slag pots, and furnace fronts. A surface that looks dry can hold water underneath a plate or inside a cavity.

Before a ladle or tundish enters the area, inspect the route for puddles, leaking hoses, open drains, and wet refractory patches. Stop the movement if any are found. Do not direct a water jet at hot metal, glowing slag, or a hot ladle to cool it faster. Use only the approved cooling method and distance.

Personnel must remain behind shields and outside the marked splash and breakout zones. Face shields and heat PPE are required where the task assessment says so, but PPE does not make standing under a ladle acceptable. Keep escape routes open. Do not park a vehicle or material trolley in the only clear path.

If water enters a hot-metal vessel or a breakout area, warn others, move to the designated safe location, and call the area controller. Do not investigate by leaning over the vessel. Report the source of water, the location, and whether any person may be exposed. During monsoon, use the weather hold point; production urgency is never a reason to accept wet charge or wet refractory.
