Roof work in a steel plant requires a permit, structural assessment, weather check, access plan, and rescue arrangement. Do not step onto translucent panels, corroded sheets, skylights, or roof areas not confirmed as load-bearing. A panel can support dust but not a person.

Use the designated walkway, guardrails, travel restraint, or fall-arrest system. Connect to rated anchors and keep away from roof edges, openings, exhaust plumes, and crane rails. Tools and materials must be secured against wind.

Stop during lightning, heavy rain, strong wind, poor visibility, or when the roof becomes slippery. Coordinate with operations because roof work above furnaces, ladle routes, and electrical yards can expose people below to falling objects or process hazards.

The rescue plan must specify how a suspended person will be reached. Do not rely on a mobile crane or an improvised rope. On completion, account for panels, tools, fasteners, and temporary covers. A roof job is not complete until every opening is secured.
