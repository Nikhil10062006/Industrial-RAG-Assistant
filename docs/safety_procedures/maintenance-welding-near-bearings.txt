Before welding near a motor, bearing, gearbox, instrument, or rotating assembly, isolate electrical and mechanical energy and establish the approved welding-return path. Do not allow welding current to pass through bearings, encoder shafts, hydraulic rods, or instrumentation. Disconnect sensitive electronics where required and place the return clamp close to the weld. Clean oil and combustible material from the area and control sparks, fumes, and hot surfaces. Verify fire watch and post-work inspection. Record equipment tag, isolation, return location, welding process, and inspection. A bearing can be damaged by current even when no visible mark appears. Do not weld on a pressurized, contaminated, or unknown material. After welding, check alignment, coating, insulation, and nearby cables before restoring service.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
