When a utility-building sump reaches high level, keep people away from wet electrical areas and do not enter the sump or switchroom to start equipment manually. Identify the source, pump status, drain route, and electrical boundary from a dry safe location. If the automatic sump pump fails, isolate power only through the approved electrical procedure and use a suitable temporary pump under authorization. Water may contain oil, chemicals, or contaminated scale. Record level, source, pump alarms, rainfall, drain condition, and affected equipment. Do not walk through standing water where cables, panels, or motor terminals may be energized. A sump alarm is often a precursor to a larger electrical or environmental event. After dewatering, inspect insulation, cable glands, earthing, floor drains, and pump check valves before restoring the area.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
