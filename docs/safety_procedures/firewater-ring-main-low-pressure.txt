A low-pressure alarm on the firewater ring main is treated as a safety-system impairment. Notify the fire-and-safety controller, check pump status, tank level, valve lineup, hydrant leakage, and test connections, and restrict hot work or other activities that rely on fire protection until the impairment is assessed. Do not open random hydrants to raise or lower pressure. If a pump is started, confirm suction, discharge, relief, and electrical condition. A closed sectional valve can isolate a large area while the main header gauge remains acceptable. Record pressure, time, pump state, tank level, affected ring section, and compensating controls. Temporary fire watch, portable extinguishers, or work suspension may be required. Firewater equipment is not ordinary utility equipment; maintenance must be planned so the protected area is known throughout the outage.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
