Lockout-tagout begins with identifying every energy source, not only the main electrical isolator. For a mill stand, include main drive electrical supply, hydraulic accumulators, coolant pressure, pneumatic lines, gravity from raised equipment, stored strip tension, roll-turning devices, and any adjacent stand that can move the workpiece.

The authorized person isolates each source, applies a personal lock and tag, and proves zero energy using the approved test method. Electrical absence-of-voltage testing must be done with a correctly rated instrument. Hydraulic pressure must be checked at the designated test point and bled to the safe tank. Blocking or pinning must be installed where gravity could move a roll, guard, or cylinder.

Try the normal start command after people are clear, then return controls to off. The test is part of proving isolation; it is not permission to enter. Every person working on the equipment applies an individual lock. A group lockbox is used when the job has several trades or shifts.

Before removal, inspect for tools and personnel, reinstall guards, and notify affected operators. Each person removes their own lock. If a lock owner is unavailable, the site’s documented removal process applies; another worker must not simply cut it. Record the isolation points and any abnormal stored energy found during the job.
