A hydraulic grab leaking near scrap piles must be isolated before a person approaches. Keep people outside the swing and drop zones, lower or secure the grab, and control the spill. Do not search for a pinhole by hand. Record pressure, hose, fluid, load, and position. Scrap can shift when the grab loses pressure. Inspect adjacent hoses and the attachment pins before returning to service. Wet oily scrap also increases slip and fire risk.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
