Emergency showers and eyewashes must be reachable without passing through the hazard that caused the exposure. Check access, signage, lighting, water flow, temperature, drain condition, nozzle covers, and test record. Remove pallets, hoses, drums, and temporary barriers that block the approach. Do not store tools or chemicals in the shower footprint. During a test, keep people clear of the discharge and report rusty, cloudy, or low-flow water. Record station ID, test time, flow condition, defect, and corrective owner. A shower that works but is hidden behind a closed gate is not an effective emergency control. New workers should walk from each chemical task to the nearest station before starting. If a shower is out of service, provide the approved temporary control and suspend high-risk work when required.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
