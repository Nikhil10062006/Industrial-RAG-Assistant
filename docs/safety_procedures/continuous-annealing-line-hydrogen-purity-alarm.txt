Treat a hydrogen-purity or oxygen-intrusion alarm as a process and fire hazard. Follow the furnace atmosphere procedure, reduce or stop the line as required, and prevent unauthorized access to the furnace vestibules. Do not open doors to “let the gas out.” Check analyzer validity, sample flow, seal condition, gas pressure, dew point, and recent maintenance from safe locations. Keep ignition sources controlled and use the required gas detector. If a leak or seal failure is suspected, isolate only under the approved sequence because rapid pressure changes can draw air into the furnace. Record alarm time, analyzer value, sample-line status, furnace pressure, door condition, and strip state. A false analyzer alarm is possible, but it must be proven through independent testing before production continues. Hydrogen service requires clean, leak-tight equipment and disciplined purge. The safest response is controlled stabilization, not improvisation.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
