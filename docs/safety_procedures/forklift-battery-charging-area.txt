In a forklift battery charging area, keep ignition sources, smoking, and unapproved electrical equipment away. Ensure ventilation is operating before charging. Inspect battery, cables, connector, electrolyte, charger setting, and emergency eyewash. Park the forklift with brake applied, forks lowered, and charger connected correctly. Do not disconnect under load or place metal tools across terminals. If bubbling is excessive, the battery heats, or electrolyte spills, stop charging and follow the chemical response. Record battery number, charger, voltage, temperature, and defect. Keep acid and alkali handling materials separate according to site practice. A charging room can accumulate hydrogen even when no smell is present. Good housekeeping around chargers prevents both electrical and chemical incidents.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
