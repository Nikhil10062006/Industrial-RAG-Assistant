Before using a sling, inspect identification tag, rated capacity, stitching, wire-rope condition, hooks, shackles, latches, abrasion, cuts, heat damage, chemical attack, and deformation. Confirm the lifting angle and load weight; capacity decreases as the angle becomes shallow. Do not use a sling with a missing tag or unknown history. Keep slings away from sharp edges using approved protection and never tie a knot to shorten one. Take the load just clear of the support to check balance, then stop if it tilts or the gear shifts. Do not stand in the bight or under a suspended load. Record gear number, load, configuration, inspection, and defect. Veteran riggers reject gear early because a small cut or flattened wire can become a failure under shock loading. Store slings dry, protected, and off the floor after use.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
