If slag rises unexpectedly in the runner or approaches the marked freeboard, warn the cast-house team and move people away from the low side and splash direction. Do not attempt to break a crust by standing beside the stream. Confirm whether the cause is a blocked slag runner, abnormal slag volume, ladle position, or a change in iron-slag separation. The area controller should stop non-essential movement and keep the crane route clear. Water hoses and wet tools must remain away from hot material. If a diversion path exists, use it only under the approved sequence; do not open an unverified gate. Watch for steam, cracking refractory, and flow under the runner shell. A runner can fail after the overflow appears to stop because heat and pressure continue to work through a weakened section. Record the level, location, cast time, slag condition, valve positions, and people exposed. After cooling and isolation, inspect refractory, supports, drains, and the skimmer. The lesson for new operators is that a rising runner is a process and a refractory hazard at the same time.
