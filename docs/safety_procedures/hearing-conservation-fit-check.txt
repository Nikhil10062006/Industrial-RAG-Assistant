Before entering a high-noise zone, verify earplug insertion or earmuff seal, compatibility with helmet and glasses, and communication method. Test fit where the program requires. Do not remove protection to hear a radio; use the approved system. Record failed plugs, damaged muffs, and noisy tasks. Hearing protection works only when worn continuously in the exposure area. Report ringing ears or sudden noise events.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
