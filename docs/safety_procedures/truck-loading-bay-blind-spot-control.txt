Before loading a truck, confirm vehicle identity, driver briefing, wheel chocks, loading plan, load weight, route, and pedestrian exclusion. The driver must remain in the designated safe area unless the procedure requires otherwise. Use one signalman and a clear radio or hand-signal method. Do not walk behind the truck or between the truck and loading equipment. Check that coils, plates, or bundles are secured for the road condition and that straps are protected from sharp edges. Record truck, material, weight, restraints, and inspection. Rain, darkness, and dust reduce visibility; stop if the signalman cannot see the load or driver. A truck that is stationary can still move when the load shifts. Release only after the documents and physical load agree.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
