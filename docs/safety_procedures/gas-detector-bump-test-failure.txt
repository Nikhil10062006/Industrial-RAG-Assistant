Before using a portable gas detector for entry or field testing, check battery, sensor date, calibration status, physical condition, filters, tubing, and bump-test result. A detector that fails the bump test is removed from service and clearly tagged. Do not continue because a second detector is inconvenient. Confirm the test gas identity and expiry, flow regulator, response, and alarm. Record instrument ID, gas concentration, result, date, and person testing. A zero reading in clean air does not prove sensor response. Detectors exposed to water, dust, solvent, or high gas concentration may need service even if they later appear normal. Carry the detector in a position where it samples breathing air and is not covered by clothing. Gas testing is a control only when the instrument is fit for use and the sample location is correct.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
