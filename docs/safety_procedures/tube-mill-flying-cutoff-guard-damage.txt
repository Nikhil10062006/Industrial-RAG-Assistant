If a flying-cutoff guard is damaged, stop the line and prevent access to the cutter path. Do not operate at reduced speed with a missing panel. Isolate cutter drive, hydraulic energy, and stored tube movement before inspection. Record guard, damage, speed, cut mode, and temporary barrier. The guard protects against fragments, tube movement, and knife contact. Repair and test interlocks before production. A small crack can become a missing panel when vibration increases.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
