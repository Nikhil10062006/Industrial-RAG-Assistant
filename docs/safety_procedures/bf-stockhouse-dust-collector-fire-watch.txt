When a dust collector temperature or spark alarm appears, stop material feed as directed and establish the exclusion zone. Check temperature, fan status, hopper level, pulse air, and fire-system readiness from outside. Do not open the collector immediately; oxygen entry can worsen a smoldering event. Isolate ignition sources and follow the dust-fire response. Record alarm tag, temperature trend, material, fan state, and extinguishing action. A collector can reignite after apparent cooling, so post-event monitoring is required.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
