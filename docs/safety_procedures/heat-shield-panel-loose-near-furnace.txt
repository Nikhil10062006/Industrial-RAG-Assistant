If a radiant-heat shield or guard panel is loose, stop work in the exposure area and keep people outside the marked boundary. Do not hold the panel in place by hand or continue because the furnace is operating normally. Notify the area controller and assess whether the panel can fall, expose hot surfaces, or redirect heat toward a walkway. Isolate or cool the equipment before repair as required. Record panel location, support condition, furnace state, temperature, and temporary barrier. A loose shield can become a falling object or allow a person to receive a heat load that was not in the task assessment. Repair the support, inspect adjacent panels, and verify the escape route before reopening access. Heat PPE does not compensate for a failed barrier.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
