If the recoiled strip begins to telescope, loosen, or collapse, stop the line using the approved sequence and keep people outside the coil path. Do not approach the coil with a bar or hand tool while the mandrel or strip may still move. Confirm line speed, tension, mandrel expansion, side guide position, and strip tracking from the safe panel. A coil can spring outward after tension is released. Isolate the mandrel, bridle, and strip movement before any correction. Use chocks and the designated handling method after the coil is secured. Record coil identity, width, tension, mandrel pressure, defect location, and time. Common causes include poor head engagement, uneven mandrel expansion, damaged separator, wrong width setup, or oil contamination. New operators often try to push the outer wraps back into place; that is not an acceptable correction. Quality and maintenance must review the coil before movement.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
