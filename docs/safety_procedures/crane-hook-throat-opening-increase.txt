If hook-throat opening, latch condition, twist, or wear is outside the approved limit, remove the hook from service immediately. Secure any load first through the lifting plan. Do not grind, heat, weld, or straighten a damaged hook. Record hook number, load, measurement, defect, and quarantine. A hook can look strong while its geometry has changed. Replacement requires inspection and proof testing as specified.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
