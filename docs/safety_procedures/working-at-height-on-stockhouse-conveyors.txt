Any work where a person could fall requires the approved platform, guardrail, or fall-arrest system. Do not climb a conveyor frame, hopper, or handrail because the access ladder is inconvenient. A dust-covered rung can be more dangerous than a visibly damaged one.

Inspect the scaffold or platform tag, grating, toe boards, gates, anchor points, and lighting before use. Wear a full-body harness where required and connect only to a rated anchor. Keep the lanyard clear of sharp edges, hot surfaces, and moving belts. Tools must be secured; a spanner falling onto a running conveyor can become a projectile.

The conveyor must be isolated if hands, feet, tools, or clothing could reach the moving parts. Do not work above an open belt while people are below. Establish a lower-level exclusion zone and use a spotter where materials are lifted.

Wind, rain, and poor visibility change the risk. Stop outdoor height work during lightning or when the platform becomes slippery. If a person falls into a suspended harness, rescue must be prompt because suspension intolerance can develop. Call the site emergency number, use the planned rescue kit, and do not improvise by moving the person with a crane unless the rescue plan specifically allows it.
