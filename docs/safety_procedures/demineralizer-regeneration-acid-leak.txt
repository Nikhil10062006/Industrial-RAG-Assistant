If acid or caustic leaks during regeneration, stop transfer from the safe control point, keep people away, and activate the chemical response. Do not approach a valve pit without the specified PPE and gas or splash assessment. Use bunding and the approved spill kit; do not wash into drains. Record chemical, tank, pressure, valve state, quantity, and exposure. The regeneration sequence must be paused until the line is isolated, flushed, inspected, and released by the responsible authority.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
