At the start of shift, check emergency radios for charge, channel, volume, microphone, spare battery, and area coverage. Test communication at known weak points such as furnace platforms, pits, and crane bays. A radio that works beside the control room may fail behind steel structures. Record defective units and issue a tested replacement before the task starts. During an emergency, use the designated channel and repeat critical messages. If communication is lost, stop movement and use the backup method, such as runner, alarm, or fixed phone. Do not continue a lifting, gas, confined-space, or hot-metal task with no reliable communication. Keep radios away from heat, water, and chemical splash. Battery discipline is part of the emergency barrier.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
