If a waste-oil drum or tank overflows, stop filling, keep people away from the spill, and use the bund or spill-control materials. Do not wash oil into a drain. Check the source hose, pump, level indicator, and receiving capacity from a safe position. Control ignition sources and vehicle traffic. Label and segregate contaminated absorbent as required. Record waste type, estimated quantity, container ID, source, containment, and environmental notification. A level indicator may be stuck or the receiving drum may be mislabeled, so verify both physical and system identity. Do not mix incompatible waste streams to make space. After cleanup, inspect the bund, drain valve, hose, and overfill alarm. Repeated overflows require a transfer procedure or capacity change, not only more absorbent.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
