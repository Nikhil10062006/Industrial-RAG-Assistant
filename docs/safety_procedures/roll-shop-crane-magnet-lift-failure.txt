If a lifting magnet loses power, alarms, or holding capacity, stop all movement and keep people outside the drop zone. Do not place hands on the roll or attempt to “catch” it. The operator should lower the load only if the approved emergency sequence confirms a safe landing; otherwise maintain the safe state and call the lifting supervisor. Check power, battery backup where fitted, magnet face cleanliness, roll temperature, and contact area from outside the zone. Scale, oil, a curved surface, or an undersized magnet can reduce holding force. Never lift a roll with unknown weight or use a magnet beyond its rated temperature. Record load, magnet number, alarm, power state, surface condition, and landing action. A magnet that held the previous roll is not proven for the next one. The lifting plan must define a clear exclusion zone and a secondary method for securing the load when required.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
