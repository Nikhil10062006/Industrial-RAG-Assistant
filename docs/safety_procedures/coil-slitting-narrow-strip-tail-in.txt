If a narrow slit tail begins to whip, wander, or wrap near the recoiler, stop the line and move people outside the strip path. Do not grab the tail or place a hand near the knives, arbor, or pinch rolls. Use the emergency stop or defined tail-out sequence, then isolate stored strip tension before clearing. Narrow strip can behave like a spring and cut through gloves. Check knife condition, separator stack, tension, recoiler alignment, and strip edge quality after the line is safe. Record the coil, slit width, speed, tension, tail location, and first abnormal movement. A tail that looks slow can accelerate when tension releases. Keep guards and containment in place; do not remove them to improve visibility during the upset. New operators should call the senior slitter operator immediately rather than trying to guide the strip by hand.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
