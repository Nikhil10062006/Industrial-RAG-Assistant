If a scrap radiation monitor alarms, stop movement of the vehicle or load and keep people outside the marked exclusion zone. Do not search the scrap by hand or move the load to “see where the alarm goes.” Notify the radiation-safety responsible person and follow the site response plan. Record vehicle, load, alarm value, location, time, and weather without approaching unnecessarily. Establish a controlled area and prevent other vehicles from passing through. The source, if present, must be located and handled only by trained personnel with the approved instrument and shielding. Do not discard suspect material into another pile. A false alarm is possible because of detector condition or background, but the load is treated as suspect until released. Maintain traceability and inform the next shift if the load remains isolated.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
