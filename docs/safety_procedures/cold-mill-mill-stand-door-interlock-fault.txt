If a stand-door interlock fails, stop and isolate the stand before inspection. Do not defeat the switch because the door appears closed. Check actuator alignment, target, cable, guard, and safety relay from the approved position. Record tag, switch state, mechanical gap, test, and repair. A door interlock protects against unexpected roll movement, stored strip tension, and coolant release. The line returns only after the complete safety circuit is tested, not when the alarm disappears.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
