Before entering a confined space, verify ventilation fan direction, duct placement, air changes, power supply, and gas-test results. A fan running backward or drawing from the space instead of supplying it can worsen conditions. Use smoke or the approved airflow indication from outside; do not enter to check. Test oxygen, flammable gas, carbon monoxide, and site-specific contaminants at top, middle, and bottom. Keep the attendant at the entry and maintain continuous communication. If the fan stops, the gas monitor alarms, or readings change, order immediate exit and suspend entry. Record fan ID, direction, test values, start time, duct position, and next test. Do not use an ordinary fan where the atmosphere may be flammable. Ventilation is a control, not a substitute for isolation and monitoring.

Before starting or restarting, confirm the responsible area owner, the boundary, the communication method, and the emergency route. Record the condition before the intervention and the result after it. Do not rely on a screen indication alone when a field check is possible. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, or visibility changes, stop and reassess rather than continuing on the original assumption. The next shift should receive the equipment tag, time, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and the condition that requires a further stop.
