When the site alarm sounds, stop work safely and move by the designated route to the muster point. Do not collect tools, return for a vehicle, or use a shortcut through a gas or crane area. If you are operating equipment, leave it in the defined safe state only if this can be done without delay or exposure.

Supervisors and contractors must account for people using the current attendance list, permit register, and visitor log. Report missing, injured, or last-known-location information to the incident controller. Do not enter the affected area to search unless assigned and equipped as part of the emergency response team.

Keep roads and access gates clear for fire, medical, and rescue vehicles. Do not leave the muster point until released. If the alarm is a drill, treat it with the same discipline; drills reveal missing people and blocked routes before a real event.

After the event, record alarm time, route problems, blocked exits, communication gaps, and unaccounted persons. A muster point that is downwind of a gas source or too close to hot-metal movement must be reviewed. Accountability is a process, not a headcount guess.
