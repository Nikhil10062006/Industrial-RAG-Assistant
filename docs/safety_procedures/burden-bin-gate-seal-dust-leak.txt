A dust leak at a burden-bin gate requires the area to be controlled before inspection. Stop nearby non-essential work, use the required respiratory protection, and check whether the leak comes from a failed seal, misalignment, overfilled bin, or damaged chute. Do not place hands near the gate or use compressed air to blow dust from clothing. Isolate the feeder and stored material before opening access. Record bin number, material, dust condition, level, and repair. Fine material can obscure moving parts and create combustible dust concerns.  Record the area, equipment, readings, people informed, temporary controls, and clearance authority. Do not rely on a green screen indication when a field check is required. If weather, heat, gas, pressure, movement, visibility, or communication changes, stop and reassess. PPE supports the control but does not replace isolation, barriers, ventilation, or a safe escape route. The handover should state the current operating mode, the last stable reading, the open risk, and the person or discipline responsible for the next check. If a temporary measure is used, give it an owner and review time. Do not leave a condition described only as “normal now”; explain what was observed, what evidence supports the decision, and what change would require stopping the equipment. This is how practical experience becomes usable plant knowledge without weakening the formal control system.
